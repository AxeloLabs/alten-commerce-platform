== How use cases are written

.Strategy question
****
What strategy describes an interaction completely enough that a developer, a tester and the product owner read the same thing?
****

A use case describes one goal of one actor and *every* way the interaction can go: the path where all is well, the variations that still succeed, and the failures.
It is written in the fully dressed form; the template is in the Documentation Guide (00-01).

Conventions used in this document:

* Steps are numbered and alternate between the actor and the system. A step says *what* happens, never how it is displayed or implemented.
* An alternative flow is named after the step it leaves: `4a` is the first variation of step 4. It still reaches the goal.
* An exception flow is named the same way. It does not reach the goal; it states where the interaction stops and what the system guarantees.
* Postconditions are split in two: what holds *on success*, when the goal is reached, and what holds *in every case*, including a failure or a crash in the middle.
* Taking a payment, refunding a payment and notifying the customer are never started by a person: they are included in the use cases that need them, and their primary actor is the customer on whose behalf they run.
* The *Authorization* row states what is {allowed} and what is {denied}. A refusal is always decided by the server.
* Error codes returned by exception flows are assigned in the Error Handling Strategy (05-01).

== Authorization matrix

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes "who may do what" a table that can be tested line by line?
****

The matrix is the single reference for access control.
Each cell becomes at least one test: an {allowed} cell is tested by doing the action, a {denied} cell by attempting it and checking the refusal.

Three principles apply everywhere:

. *Deny by default.* What is not listed as allowed is denied.
. *Own data only.* A customer acts on their own cart, favorites, orders and payments. Someone else's data is answered as "not found", so that its existence is not revealed.
. *Roles do not nest.* An administrator account is not a customer account: it has no cart and places no order.
