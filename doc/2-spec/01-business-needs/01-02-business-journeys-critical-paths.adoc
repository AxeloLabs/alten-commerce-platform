== Method

.Strategy question
****
What strategy finds the few paths on which the business depends, before any technical design is drawn?
****

A senior engineer does not start from services or tables.
The work starts from what actors want to achieve, and from what happens when the system fails while they are achieving it.

. *List the journeys.* A journey is an actor's goal followed from intent to outcome, across every screen and service it touches.
. *Rank them.* For each journey, estimate the damage of a failure (lost sale, customer charged for nothing, corrupted data) and how exposed it is (number of services, external dependencies, concurrency).
. *Name the critical paths.* The journeys, or branches of journeys, with the highest damage and exposure. They receive the detailed use cases, the business process diagrams, the sequence diagrams and the end-to-end tests.
. *Break each critical path on purpose.* At every step ask: what if this fails, answers twice, answers late, or never answers? Decide the state the business must end in, and how what was already done is undone.
. *Derive the rest.* Use cases, business rules, the order saga and the tests all come from that table of failures, not the other way around.

== Critical paths

.Strategy question
****
What strategy decides where the most detailed analysis and the most expensive tests are spent?
****

A path is critical when its failure costs money or trust, and when it is exposed enough for that failure to be likely.
Six paths meet both conditions.

.Selection of the critical paths
[cols="3,6,5,5", options="header"]
|===
| ID | Path | Damage if it fails | Exposure
| {CP-ORD-001} | Order placed and confirmed | No revenue | Four services, three asynchronous steps, one external provider
| {CP-ORD-002} | Order rejected: payment refused | Stock frozen, customer misinformed | Requires undoing a completed step
| {CP-ORD-003} | Order rejected: stock no longer available | Overselling, or charging for nothing | Concurrent buyers on the last units
| {CP-ORD-004} | Order outcome stays consistent under failure | Customer charged without an order, or stock lost | Crashes, time-outs, duplicate and late messages
| {CP-CAT-001} | Published product becomes purchasable | Nothing to sell, or wrong price sold | Two services kept in sync by events
| {CP-IAM-001} | Only authorized actors act | Data exposed, catalog vandalized | Every entry point
|===

[[CP-ORD-001]]
=== CP-ORD-001 — Order placed and confirmed

{critical}

The nominal purchase: the customer submits the checkout, the stock is reserved, the payment is captured, the reservation is confirmed, the order becomes `CONFIRMED`, the ordered quantities leave the cart and a confirmation e-mail is sent.

Guarantees:: The customer is charged exactly once, for exactly the order total. The stock on hand decreases by exactly the quantities ordered.
Use cases:: {UC-ORD-001} {UC-PAY-001} {UC-NOT-001} {UC-ORD-003}

plantuml::diagrams/cp-order-confirmed-sequence.puml[format=svg, align=center]

This business view is drawn for the nominal path. Every other path — refusal, missing stock, time-outs, restarts, duplicates — has its technical sequence diagram in document 03-03, one per saga scenario.

[[CP-ORD-002]]
=== CP-ORD-002 — Order rejected: payment refused

{critical}

The payment provider declines the payment.
The stock that was reserved is released, the order becomes `REJECTED` with the reason `PAYMENT_REFUSED`, the cart is kept so that the customer can try another means of payment.

Guarantees:: Nothing is charged. The reserved quantities are available again at once.
Use cases:: {UC-ORD-001} {UC-PAY-001} {UC-NOT-001}

[[CP-ORD-003]]
=== CP-ORD-003 — Order rejected: stock no longer available

{critical}

Between the moment the customer saw the product and the moment the order is placed, another customer bought the last units.
The reservation fails, the order becomes `REJECTED` with the reason `OUT_OF_STOCK`, and *payment is never requested*.

Guarantees:: When two customers compete for the last unit, exactly one order is confirmed. The other customer is never charged.
Use cases:: {UC-ORD-001} {UC-CART-002}

[[CP-ORD-004]]
=== CP-ORD-004 — Order outcome stays consistent under failure

{critical}

Something technical goes wrong while an order is being processed: a service is down or slow, the payment provider does not answer, a message is delivered twice or late, the order service restarts in the middle.
The order still ends either `CONFIRMED` or `REJECTED`; when the system cannot decide alone, it ends `UNDER_REVIEW` and an administrator is alerted.

Guarantees:: Never a rejected order that still holds a payment. Never a confirmed order without confirmed stock. Never the same payment taken twice. Never an order left pending beyond the review delay.
Use cases:: {UC-ORD-001} {UC-PAY-001} {UC-PAY-002} {UC-ORD-007}

[[CP-CAT-001]]
=== CP-CAT-001 — Published product becomes purchasable

{critical}

The administrator creates a product, sets its price, gives it stock and publishes it.
The product appears in the storefront with the right price and the right availability, and can be added to a cart and bought.

Guarantees:: A customer never finds a draft or a retired product in the catalog; a retired product is readable only where an order, a cart or a favorite references it. The price charged is the price published when the order was placed.
Use cases:: {UC-CAT-003} {UC-CAT-005} {UC-CAT-006} {UC-INV-001} {UC-CAT-002}

[[CP-IAM-001]]
=== CP-IAM-001 — Only authorized actors act

{critical}

A visitor cannot buy. A customer cannot read or change what belongs to another customer, nor touch the catalog, the stock or other people's orders. Only an administrator administers.

Guarantees:: Every request is checked on the server, whatever the storefront shows or hides.
Use cases:: {UC-IAM-001} {UC-IAM-002}

== Failure analysis of order placement

.Strategy question
****
What strategy guarantees that an order ends in a state the business can live with, whatever fails and whenever it fails?
****

The order is placed in five steps — create it, reserve the stock, charge the payment, confirm the stock, confirm the order — spread over three services; a fourth then tells the customer.
A database transaction cannot span them, so each step commits alone and a later failure is repaired by *undoing* the earlier steps.
The order of the steps is chosen so that as little as possible has to be undone:

. the stock is reserved first, because a reservation is cheap to release;
. the payment comes after, because money is the costly thing to give back;
. once the payment is captured the order goes forward: the remaining steps are retried, and the only way back is a refund.

The table lists, for each step, what can go wrong, how the system notices, what it does, and where the order ends.

.What can fail when an order is placed
[cols="3,5,4,6,4", options="header"]
|===
| Step | What goes wrong | How it is noticed | What the system does | The order ends

| Checkout review
| A price changed, a product was retired, or the stock known to the catalog no longer covers a line
| Checked when the checkout is submitted
| Nothing is created. The customer is sent back to the cart with the differences shown.
| No order

.2+| Order creation
| The customer submits twice, or the browser retries
| Each checkout carries its own identifier
| The first order is returned; nothing new is created.
| One order

| The customer checks out again while an order is pending
| One pending order per customer
| The new checkout is refused and points to the pending order.
| One order

.2+| Stock reservation
| One line cannot be reserved in full
| Inventory answers "rejected"
| Nothing was reserved, nothing to undo. Payment is not requested.
| `REJECTED` `OUT_OF_STOCK`

| Inventory does not answer
| Order deadline reached
| No payment was requested. A release is sent in case the reservation was made after all; it would expire by itself anyway.
| `REJECTED` `TIMED_OUT`

.3+| Payment
| The provider refuses
| Payment answers "refused"
| The reservation is released.
| `REJECTED` `PAYMENT_REFUSED`

| The provider or the payment service does not answer
| Order deadline reached
| The payment is canceled at the provider, so that it can no longer be captured. Canceled: the reservation is released. Already captured: the order continues.
| `REJECTED` `TIMED_OUT`, or continues

| Nobody can say whether the payment was captured
| Review delay reached
| Automatic processing stops. An administrator is alerted and the customer is told.
| `UNDER_REVIEW`

.3+| Stock confirmation
| Inventory is unavailable
| No answer
| Retried. The payment is captured and the reservation is still held.
| `CONFIRMED` once it succeeds

| Inventory is still unavailable
| Review delay reached
| Automatic processing stops. An administrator is alerted and the customer is told.
| `UNDER_REVIEW`

| The reservation expired during a long outage and the stock is gone
| Inventory answers "cannot confirm" when an administrator retries
| The payment is refunded; only then is the order rejected.
| `REJECTED` `OUT_OF_STOCK`, refunded

| Confirmation e-mail
| The e-mail provider fails
| Send error
| Retried, then recorded as not sent. The order is not affected.
| `CONFIRMED`

.3+| Any undo
| A release does not get through
| No answer
| Nothing more is needed: the reservation expires by itself.
| Unchanged

| A refund keeps failing
| Retries exhausted
| The order is handed to an administrator, or stays with one. The payment is never recorded as refunded when it is not.
| `UNDER_REVIEW`

| The undo is delivered twice
| Each message carries its own identifier
| The second one is ignored.
| Unchanged

.3+| Any step
| The order service restarts in the middle
| On restart
| The order resumes from the last step it recorded; nothing is asked twice with a different effect.
| As if nothing happened

| A message is delivered twice, or two messages arrive together
| Each message carries its own identifier; an order handles one message at a time
| Each message has its effect once; the others are ignored.
| Unchanged

| A service saves its work and stops before telling the others
| On restart
| The pending message is sent; no business operation is lost.
| As if nothing happened
|===

With these delays the store itself never has to refund in the automatic flow: the stock is reserved before the payment, an unanswered payment is canceled before the order is rejected, and an order is decided long before its reservation can expire.
A refund remains for two cases only: a cancellation by the customer, and a decision of an administrator after a long outage.

The technical design that implements these guarantees — the orchestrated saga, the transactional outbox, idempotent consumers, deadlines — is specified in folder 03.
Each row of the table becomes a saga scenario there, with its sequence diagram and its tests.
