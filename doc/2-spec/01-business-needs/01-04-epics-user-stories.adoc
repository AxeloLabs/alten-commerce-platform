== How epics and stories are written

.Strategy question
****
What strategy turns business journeys into a backlog that can be prioritized, estimated and accepted?
****

An *epic* is a capability of the store, too large to deliver at once; it serves one journey.
A *user story* is the smallest increment of that capability that is worth something to an actor.
Stories are what is planned, built, demonstrated and accepted.

.Rules for a user story
[%unbreakable, cols="3,11", options="header"]
|===
| Rule | Meaning
| One actor, one benefit | "As a _role_, I want _capability_ so that _benefit_." A story with no benefit is a task.
| INVEST | Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable. A story that fails one of them is rewritten or split.
| Acceptance criteria | Two to five observable outcomes. They are refined into the acceptance scenarios of document 01-08, where every business rule behind a criterion has at least one scenario.
| Priority | MoSCoW. {must}: the release has no value without it. {should}: expected, can slip. {could}: only if time allows. {wont}: explicitly excluded.
| Estimate | Story points on the Fibonacci scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8). Relative size, not days. A story above 8 is split.
| Realized by | The use case that details every flow of the story, in document 01-05.
|===

A story is *ready* to be built when its acceptance criteria are agreed, its use case is approved and its acceptance scenarios exist.
It is *done* when those scenarios pass, the quality gates are green and the product owner accepted it.
Both definitions are detailed in document 08-04.

[[EPIC-IAM-001]]
== EPIC-IAM-001 — Account and access

[cols="2,11"]
|===
| Journey | {JRN-IAM-001}
| Value | A visitor becomes a known customer; the store knows who may do what.
| Scope | Registration, sign-in, sign-out, the two roles `CUSTOMER` and `ADMIN`. Credentials are handled by the identity provider.
| Owner | {role-sec} {role-swe}
|===

[[US-IAM-001]]
[cols="3,11", frame=all, grid=rows, stripes=none]
|===
2+h| US-IAM-001 — Register as a customer
| Epic | {EPIC-IAM-001}
| Story | *As a* visitor, *I want* to create an account with my e-mail address and a password *so that* I can keep a cart and buy.
| Priority | {must} · 3 points
| Acceptance criteria a|
. With a valid e-mail address and a password that meets the policy, the account is created with the role `CUSTOMER`.
. An e-mail address already registered is refused.
. A password that does not meet the policy is refused, and the policy is shown.
. After registering, the visitor can sign in at once.
| Realized by | {UC-IAM-001}
|===

[[US-IAM-002]]
[cols="3,11", frame=all, grid=rows, stripes=none]
|===
2+h| US-IAM-002 — Sign in and sign out
| Epic | {EPIC-IAM-001}
| Story | *As a* customer or an administrator, *I want* to sign in and sign out *so that* my cart, my orders and my rights are mine alone.
| Priority | {must} · 2 points
| Acceptance criteria a|
. Valid credentials sign the actor in; the storefront shows who is signed in.
. Invalid credentials are refused with one message that does not say which part was wrong.
. Signing out ends the session; a protected page then leads to the sign-in page.
| Realized by | {UC-IAM-002}
|===

[[US-IAM-003]]
[cols="3,11", frame=all, grid=rows, stripes=none]
|===
2+h| US-IAM-003 — Reach only what my role allows
| Epic | {EPIC-IAM-001}
| Story | *As an* administrator, *I want* each function to be reserved to the roles entitled to it *so that* nobody can alter the store or read someone else's data.
| Priority | {must} · 3 points
| Acceptance criteria a|
. A visitor who calls a customer function is asked to sign in.
. A customer who calls an administration function is refused, even by typing its address directly.
. A customer who asks for another customer's cart, favorites or order is answered as if it did not exist.
. The refusal is decided by the server, whatever the storefront displays.
| Realized by | {UC-IAM-002}
|===

| Epic | {EPIC-PAY-001}

| Priority | {must} · 5 points
