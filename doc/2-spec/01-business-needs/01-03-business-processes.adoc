== How processes are drawn

.Strategy question
****
What strategy shows the business how work flows between people and services, in a notation it can read and challenge?
****

A business process diagram answers three questions that a journey sheet and a use case do not answer together: *who* does each step, *in which order*, and *where the flow forks*.
Processes are drawn in BPMN 2.0, the standard notation for business processes, restricted to the handful of symbols below so that anyone can read them after one minute.

.The notation used
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Symbol | Meaning
| Thin circle | Start event: what triggers the process.
| Thick circle | End event: one possible outcome. A process has as many end events as it has outcomes.
| Rounded box with a person | A task done by a person.
| Rounded box with a gear | A task done by the system.
| Rounded box with an envelope | A message sent to someone outside the process, here an e-mail.
| Diamond with a cross | Exclusive choice: exactly one of the outgoing paths is taken.
| Horizontal band | Lane: the actor or the bounded context responsible for the tasks it contains.
|===

Each lane of the platform is a bounded context, and will be a service.
A flow that crosses from one lane to another is therefore an exchange between services: the diagrams show the business reason for every such exchange before any technical design.

Processes are drawn for the nominal flow and the business alternatives.
Technical failures — a service that does not answer, a duplicated message, a restart — are deliberately absent: they are analyzed in document 01-02 and specified as saga scenarios in folder 03.

The diagrams are generated from text and are valid BPMN 2.0 files: they open in Camunda Modeler or any BPMN tool.

== Order placement

.Strategy question
****
What strategy orders the steps of an order so that a failure leaves as little as possible to undo?
****

[[BPMN-ORD-001]]
=== BPMN-ORD-001 — Order placement

{critical}

The process realizes the journey {JRN-ORD-001} and the use case {UC-ORD-001}.
Its order is the business decision that shapes the whole platform: *the stock is reserved before the payment is taken*.
At the first fork, a cart that changed creates nothing. At the second, a missing stock leaves nothing to undo. At the third, a refused payment costs one release. Once the payment is captured the order goes forward, and the only way back is a refund.

.Steps of the order placement
[cols="1,3,7,4", options="header"]
|===
| # | Lane | Step | Rules
| 1 | Customer | Submits the checkout: lines reviewed, shipping address, payment token. | {FR-ORD-001}
| 2 | Order | Checks that prices and stock are those the customer reviewed. If they are, creates the order `PENDING` and acknowledges it. | {FR-ORD-002} {FR-ORD-003} {FR-ORD-009} {FR-ORD-017}
| 3 | Inventory | Reserves the stock of every line, all or nothing. | {FR-INV-003} {FR-ORD-006}
| 4 | Payment | Charges the order total, once. | {FR-PAY-001} {FR-PAY-002}
| 5 | Inventory | Confirms the reservation: the stock on hand decreases. Or releases it when the payment was refused. | {FR-INV-005}
| 6 | Order | Sets the order `CONFIRMED` and removes the ordered quantities from the cart. Or sets it `REJECTED` with its reason. | {FR-ORD-007} {FR-ORD-008} {FR-CART-007}
| 7 | Notification | Sends the confirmation or the rejection e-mail. | {FR-NOT-001} {FR-NOT-003}
|===

== Product publication

.Strategy question
****
What strategy takes a product from an idea of the administrator to something a customer can buy, across two services?
****

[[BPMN-CAT-001]]
=== BPMN-CAT-001 — Product publication

{critical}

The process realizes the journey {JRN-CAT-002} and the critical path {CP-CAT-001}, through the use cases {UC-CAT-003}, {UC-INV-001} and {UC-CAT-006}.
The catalog owns the product and its price; the inventory owns its stock.
Neither asks the other: the inventory announces each change of availability and the catalog listens ({FR-INV-007}).

The administrator may add stock before or after publishing.
A product published with no stock is shown as out of stock, which is correct ({FR-CAT-008}).

== Order cancellation and refund

.Strategy question
****
What strategy undoes a confirmed order without ever telling the customer they were refunded when they were not?
****

[[BPMN-ORD-002]]
=== BPMN-ORD-002 — Order cancellation and refund

{st-proposed}

The process realizes the journey {JRN-ORD-003} and the use case {UC-ORD-004}.
The order is `CANCELING` from the moment the cancellation is accepted, so that it can no longer ship, and becomes `CANCELED` only after the refund is made ({FR-ORD-012}).
A refund that keeps failing does not end the process silently: the order goes under review and a person takes over ({FR-ORD-014}, {UC-ORD-007}).

{st-proposed}

The process realizes the journey {JRN-ORD-004} and the use case {UC-ORD-006}.
