== Vision

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps a small e-commerce product focused on what matters to the business?
****

The {project-name} is an online store where customers buy products from a catalog managed by an administrator.
The business lives or dies on one promise: *an order that is confirmed is paid and its stock is secured; an order that cannot be honored costs the customer nothing*.

Everything in this specification is ranked against that promise.
Placing an order is the critical journey; browsing, carts and favorites feed it; notifications and order tracking report on it.

.Business objectives
[cols="1,6,7", options="header"]
|===
| # | Objective | How we know it is met
| 1 | A customer finds a product and buys it without help | The journey {JRN-ORD-001} completes end to end in a few clicks
| 2 | The store never sells what it does not have | No confirmed order without confirmed stock ({FR-ORD-007})
| 3 | A customer is never charged for nothing | A rejected order never holds a payment ({FR-ORD-008})
| 4 | The administrator runs the catalog alone | Products, prices and stock are managed without a developer ({JRN-CAT-002}, {JRN-INV-001})
| 5 | The customer always knows where an order stands | Status on screen and by e-mail at every change ({JRN-ORD-002})
| 6 | A failure degrades the store, it does not corrupt it | Every order ends in a consistent state, whatever fails ({CP-ORD-004})
|===

== Actors

.Strategy question
****
What strategy tells us who the system serves and which other systems it depends on?
****

An actor is anyone or anything outside the platform that interacts with it.
Human actors have goals; system actors provide a service the platform relies on.

.Human actors
[%unbreakable, cols="3,3,9", options="header"]
|===
| Actor | Authenticated | Description and goals
| Visitor | No | Anyone on the storefront who is not signed in. Browses the catalog, views products, registers, signs in.
| Customer | Yes, role `CUSTOMER` | A registered person. Does everything a visitor does, keeps a cart and favorites, places orders, pays, follows orders.
| Administrator | Yes, role `ADMIN` | Runs the store. Manages products, prices and stock, follows all orders, resolves blocked orders.
|===

.System actors
[%unbreakable, cols="3,3,9", options="header"]
|===
| Actor | Nature | Role
| Identity provider | Off-the-shelf product, deployed with the platform | Registers accounts, authenticates people, issues tokens that carry the role.
| Payment provider | External service, *simulated* in this release | Accepts or refuses a payment, refunds it. Its behavior is scriptable so that every scenario can be reproduced.
| E-mail provider | External service | Delivers the e-mails sent by the platform.
| Time | The clock | Triggers what must happen without anyone asking: a stock reservation expires, an order that received no answer is rejected.
|===

== Scope

.Strategy question
****
What strategy prevents the scope from growing until nothing is finished?
****

The first release covers one complete purchase loop and the administration needed to feed it.
Anything that does not exercise a new business rule or a new architectural pattern is left out, and listed here so that the omission is a decision, not an oversight.

.In scope
[cols="4,9,3", options="header"]
|===
| Capability | Content | Journey
| Account and access | Registration, sign-in, sign-out, two roles | {JRN-IAM-001}
| Product discovery | Catalog browsing, search and filters, product page with availability | {JRN-CAT-001}
| Cart | Add, change, remove, cart counter, cart kept between sessions | {JRN-CART-001}
| Favorites | Mark, unmark, list, add to cart from the list | {JRN-CART-002}
| Order placement | Checkout, stock reservation, simulated payment, confirmation or rejection | {JRN-ORD-001}
| Order tracking | Order history, order detail and status, e-mails | {JRN-ORD-002}
| Catalog administration | Create, update, price, publish and retire products | {JRN-CAT-002}
| Stock administration | Adjust stock, review stock levels | {JRN-INV-001}
| Order supervision and recovery | An administrator finds any order, and decides those the system could not decide alone | {JRN-ORD-005}
|===

.Proposed, awaiting validation
[cols="4,9,3", options="header"]
|===
| Capability | Content | Journey
| Order cancellation | A customer cancels a confirmed order before it ships and is refunded | {JRN-ORD-003}
| Order fulfillment | The administrator marks an order shipped, then delivered | {JRN-ORD-004}
|===

.Out of scope for this release
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Excluded | Reason
| Guest checkout | A cart and an order belong to an account. Buying requires signing in.
| Real payment provider | Payment is simulated behind a port; a real provider is a later adapter, not a redesign.
| Shipping fees, taxes, promotions | Prices include VAT and shipping is free: order amounts stay simple, the saga does not.
| Returns after delivery | Same pattern as cancellation; adds a second saga without teaching anything new.
| Reviews, ratings, recommendations | No link with the order lifecycle.
| Several currencies, languages or warehouses | One currency (EUR), one language (English), one stock per product.
| Invoices and accounting exports | Downstream of the order; no impact on its consistency.
| Supplier purchasing and replenishment | Stock arrives through manual adjustment by the administrator.
| Password reset, profile, e-mail verification | Offered by the standard pages of the identity provider and not specified here. A new account can sign in at once.
| Saved addresses, delivery areas | The shipping address is typed at checkout, as free text, and kept with the order. Nothing checks that it can be delivered.
| Product images | A product has a name and a description. Pictures add storage and nothing to the order.
| Category management | Categories are a fixed list, set when the store is installed.
|===

== Constraints and assumptions

.Strategy question
****
What strategy separates what is imposed from what we chose, so that nobody re-opens a constraint by mistake?
****

.Imposed constraints
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Constraint | Consequence
| Microservices | The platform is split into independently deployable services, one per bounded context.
| Spring Boot back end, Angular front end | Fixed for every service and for the storefront.
| Docker and Kubernetes on GKE Autopilot | Every service is a container; the platform runs on Google Cloud.
| Google Cloud Pub/Sub as the only message bus | Asynchronous communication uses Pub/Sub; no Kafka.
| CloudEvents and Protocol Buffers | Every message follows the CloudEvents convention with a Protobuf payload.
| REST for synchronous exchanges | No GraphQL.
| Terraform, Secret Manager | Infrastructure is code; secrets live in Secret Manager.
| English | Code, identifiers and documentation are in English.
| No state machine engine | Order states are modeled in domain code.
|===

.Working assumptions
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Assumption | If it proves wrong
| A cart and favorites require a signed-in customer | A visitor cart would be added in the storefront and merged at sign-in.
| One line holds at most 10 units; a cart holds at most 50 lines | The limits are configuration values ({FR-CART-002}).
| A stock reservation lives 15 minutes | Configuration value ({FR-INV-004}); must stay well above the order deadline.
| An order with no outcome after 2 minutes is rejected | Configuration value ({FR-ORD-015}).
| Low stock means 5 units or fewer | Configuration value ({FR-CAT-008}).
| The administrator role is granted in the identity provider | A user-administration screen would be a new journey.
|===

== Decisions awaiting validation

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps the product owner in control of the scope before detailed design starts?
****

These points are submitted at the gate of this lot.
Everything written about them in the business documents carries the badge {st-proposed}; nothing downstream depends on them yet.

[cols="1,5,6,4", options="header"]
|===
| # | Decision | Recommendation | Impact if refused
| 1 | Add order cancellation by the customer ({JRN-ORD-003}) | Accept. It is the one journey where a refund is a normal business event; without it, a refund only ever follows a decision of an administrator. | The automatic refund is never exercised.
| 2 | Add order fulfillment by the administrator ({JRN-ORD-004}) | Accept. It gives the order lifecycle its `SHIPPED` and `DELIVERED` statuses. | The lifecycle ends at `CONFIRMED`, which becomes a final status.
| 3 | Require sign-in to keep a cart | Accept. | A visitor cart must be designed in the storefront.
| 4 | Keep the limits and delays of the table "Limits and delays" in document 01-06 | Accept; they are configuration values. | Values change, design does not.
| 5 | An administrator account cannot buy: roles do not nest | Accept. It keeps the authorization matrix testable line by line. | An administrator also needs a cart, favorites and orders.
| 6 | One line that cannot be reserved rejects the whole order ({FR-INV-003}) | Accept. A partial order is a different promise to the customer. | Partial orders, partial payments and partial rejections must be designed.
| 7 | The cart counter counts units, not products ({FR-CART-006}) | Accept. | A change of wording and of one rule.
| 8 | A retired product is never put back on sale ({FR-CAT-005}) | Accept. A product that returns gets a new SKU. | Reactivation needs its own rule and its own checks on carts and favorites.
| 9 | A customer has one order pending at a time ({FR-ORD-017}) | Accept. It lasts seconds and prevents two orders from one cart. | The cart must be locked or split while an order is pending.
| 10 | A cart line that can no longer be bought blocks the checkout until it is removed ({FR-CART-010}) | Accept. The customer always sees what will not be ordered. | Such lines are skipped silently.
|===

.Decisions already taken by the product owner on 2026-11-18
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Decision
| Interchangeable persistence | PostgreSQL is the store of every service that takes part in an order. The three interchangeable stores — PostgreSQL, a document database, a file — are implemented for the catalog and the cart only, and proven by one shared test suite ({NFR-MAINT-004}).
| Environments | One Google Cloud project and one cluster, with three isolated environments: test, integration, production. The layout with one project per environment is described, not deployed.
| Documentation | Sources in AsciiDoc with diagrams as text; PDF generated by the build.
|===

== Glossary

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes the business, the documents and the code use the same word for the same thing?
****

These terms are the ubiquitous language of the platform.
They are used with this meaning, and only this meaning, in every document and in the code.

[cols="3,12", options="header"]
|===
| Term | Meaning
| Product | Something the store sells, identified by its SKU.
| SKU | Stock keeping unit: the permanent, unique business identifier of a product.
| Catalog | The set of products with their descriptions, categories and prices.
| Published, retired | A published product is visible and sellable. A retired product is no longer sold but remains readable where it is referenced.
| Stock on hand | The quantity of a product physically held.
| Reservation | A quantity of stock set aside for one order while it is being paid. It is confirmed, released, or it expires.
| Available quantity | Stock on hand minus reserved quantities: what can still be sold.
| Available quantity known to the catalog | The catalog's own copy of the available quantity, refreshed each time the inventory announces a change. It can be a few seconds late.
| Availability level | What the storefront shows, derived from that copy: in stock, low stock or out of stock. An indication, not a guarantee.
| Cart | The products a customer intends to buy, with quantities. One per customer.
| Favorite | A product a customer marked to find it again.
| Checkout | The step where the customer reviews the cart, gives a shipping address and a means of payment, and submits.
| Same checkout | The storefront gives each checkout an identifier when the review is shown. Two submissions carrying the same identifier are the same checkout, and give one order.
| Flagged line | A cart line that can no longer be bought as it is: its product was retired, or its quantity is no longer available.
| Order | A customer's commitment to buy given products at given prices. Created at checkout, immutable afterwards.
| Order number | The human-readable identifier of an order.
| Payment | The charge of an order total on the customer's means of payment.
| Payment token | An opaque value issued by the payment provider that stands for a card. The customer enters card details on the provider's form, the storefront receives the token, and the platform never sees card data.
| Refund | The return of a captured payment to the customer.
| Confirmed, rejected | An order is confirmed when it is paid and its stock is secured. It is rejected, with a reason, when it cannot be; a rejected order holds no payment.
| Settled | An order is settled when it is no longer pending: the customer has an answer.
| Under review | The status of an order the system could not decide alone. Automatic processing has stopped; an administrator decides.
| Saga | The sequence of steps, spread over several services, that takes an order to its outcome.
| Compensation, undo | The action that undoes a completed step when a later step fails: releasing stock, canceling or refunding a payment. Business documents say "undo"; technical documents say "compensation".
| Critical path | A journey, or a branch of one, whose failure stops the business or harms the customer.
|===
