== How quality attributes are written

.Strategy question
****
What strategy turns "fast", "reliable" and "secure" into numbers a test or a dashboard can check?
****

A non-functional requirement is useless until it can be measured.
Each one below states a *measure*, a *target* and the *way it is verified*: a test, a quality gate in the pipeline, or a service level objective watched in production.
A requirement with no verification is a wish and is not accepted in this document.

The attributes follow the ISO/IEC 25010 quality model, limited to those that matter for an order-management platform.
Targets are deliberately modest: they describe a small store that must be correct before it is fast.
They are the starting service level objectives; the Observability Strategy (05-03) turns them into indicators, dashboards and alerts.

.Categories
[%unbreakable, cols="2,4,9", options="header"]
|===
| Code | Category | Question
| `PERF` | Performance | Is it fast enough for the customer?
| `AVAIL` | Availability | Is it there when the customer comes?
| `REL` | Reliability and consistency | Does it stay correct when something fails?
| `SEC` | Security | Is it protected against misuse?
| `OBS` | Observability | Can we see what it does and why it failed?
| `MAINT` | Maintainability | Can we change it safely?
| `SCAL` | Scalability | Does it follow the load?
| `USAB` | Usability | Can people use it without help?
| `COST` | Cost | Does it stay within budget?
|===

Load assumption for every performance target: 100 customers browsing at the same time, 50 catalog requests per second, 2 orders per second at peak.

== Performance

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps the customer from waiting, when placing an order takes several services and an external provider?
****

The customer never waits for the whole order to be processed.
Placing an order is acknowledged at once; the outcome arrives a few seconds later.
The two are measured separately.

[cols="3,6,4,5", options="header"]
|===
| ID | Requirement | Target | Verified by
| [[NFR-PERF-001]]NFR-PERF-001 | Response time of catalog and order reads (list, search, product, order history, order detail) | 95th percentile below 300 ms | Load test; latency objective per service
| [[NFR-PERF-002]]NFR-PERF-002 | Time to acknowledge an order placement | 95th percentile below 500 ms | Load test on the checkout
| [[NFR-PERF-003]]NFR-PERF-003 | Time from order placement to its outcome, when every service is healthy | 95th percentile below 5 s, 99th below 15 s | Load test; saga duration metric
| [[NFR-PERF-004]]NFR-PERF-004 | Response time of cart and favorites operations | 95th percentile below 200 ms | Load test
|===

== Reliability and consistency

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps orders, stock and payments consistent when messages are duplicated, delayed or lost and services restart?
****

These requirements are the technical face of the critical path {CP-ORD-004}.
They are verified by the saga and resilience test scenarios of document 07-03.

[cols="3,6,4,5", options="header"]
|===
| ID | Requirement | Target | Verified by
| [[NFR-REL-001]]NFR-REL-001 | No business operation is lost: a state change and the message that announces it are saved together, and the message is eventually published even if the service stops in between | 100 % of committed changes published | Crash test between commit and publish
| [[NFR-REL-002]]NFR-REL-002 | Duplicate, late and out-of-order messages are harmless | No double charge, no double refund, no double stock movement, no double e-mail | Duplicate-delivery and concurrency tests
| [[NFR-REL-003]]NFR-REL-003 | No order stays without an outcome | Every order final or under review within 5 minutes; an alert otherwise | Deadline tests; alert on stuck orders
| [[NFR-REL-004]]NFR-REL-004 | A new version is deployed without interrupting orders in progress | Zero failed order caused by a deployment | Rolling-update test under load
| [[NFR-REL-005]]NFR-REL-005 | A committed change is published promptly | 99th percentile of publication delay below 5 s | Delay metric between commit and publish
|===

== Security

.Strategy question
****
What strategy protects customers' data and the store's integrity at every layer?
****

[cols="3,6,4,5", options="header"]
|===
| ID | Requirement | Target | Verified by
| [[NFR-SEC-001]]NFR-SEC-001 | Authentication through the identity provider with a standard protocol; every service validates the proof of identity itself | No request trusted on the word of another service | Security tests; architecture rule
| [[NFR-SEC-002]]NFR-SEC-002 | Authorization checked on the server for every request, deny by default | Every cell of the authorization matrix has a test | Authorization test suite
| [[NFR-SEC-003]]NFR-SEC-003 | Encryption in transit and at rest | TLS 1.2 or later on every external exchange; managed encryption on every data store | Configuration scan
| [[NFR-SEC-004]]NFR-SEC-004 | Secrets kept in the secret manager only | No secret in the repository, in an image or in a log | Secret scan in the pipeline
| [[NFR-SEC-005]]NFR-SEC-005 | No known serious vulnerability shipped | No critical or high vulnerability in dependencies or images at release | Dependency and image scans as quality gates
| [[NFR-SEC-006]]NFR-SEC-006 | Administration actions are audited | Who, what, when for every change to catalog, stock and orders | Audit record tests
| [[NFR-SEC-007]]NFR-SEC-007 | Personal data is minimized; logs and e-mails carry no password, token or payment data | Zero occurrence | Log review test; code review rule
| [[NFR-SEC-008]]NFR-SEC-008 | Common web attacks are mitigated | No finding of high severity in the dynamic scan | Dynamic security scan of the test environment
|===
