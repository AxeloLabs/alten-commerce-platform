== Testing a promise made under failure

.Strategy question
****
What strategy tests "it still works when something breaks" without waiting for something to break?
****

The saga specification promises outcomes under failure: an order is never charged twice, never left pending, never rejected while it holds a payment.
Such a promise is tested only by causing the failure, on purpose, at a chosen instant, and as many times as wanted.

The same scenarios are tested at three depths, from the cheapest to the most real.

.Three depths
[cols="3,5,4,4", options="header"]
|===
| Depth | What runs | Proves | Catalog
| The saga alone | The domain classes, fed with events and deadlines | Every cell of the grids; every invariant over generated sequences | {TU-ORD-005} to {TU-ORD-012}
| One service | A service with its database and the bus emulator, its neighbors replaced by scripted messages | Transactions, inbox, outbox, deadlines, restarts of that service | {TC-ORD-003} {TC-ORD-004} {TC-INV-002} {TC-PAY-001}
| Several services | The services of the saga together, with the simulated providers | The scenarios of document 03-03, end to end through the bus | {TI-ORD-001} to {TI-ORD-019}
|===

Most of the proof is at the first depth, where a thousand sequences run in a second.
The third depth proves that the pieces agree; it repeats each named scenario once, not every variation.

== Means of control

.Strategy question
****
What strategy gives a test the power to break exactly one thing at exactly one moment?
****

plantuml::diagrams/scenario-harness.puml[format=svg, align=center]

.What a scenario controls
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Need | Means
| Decide what the payment provider does | The token given at checkout scripts the simulated provider ({FR-PAY-007}): see the table below
| Make a service silent | In a composition of containers: the container is stopped, and started again later. On a deployed environment: the operations job that scales one workload to nothing (document 06-07).
| Cross a deadline | Delays are configuration values (document 01-06). Scenarios run with an order deadline of 5 s, a review delay of 15 s and a reservation lifetime of 45 s: the same order as in production, a hundredth of the wait. At the first two depths the clock itself is moved by the test.
| Deliver a message twice | The test republishes a message it observed: with the same identifier, as the bus would; or as a new message with the same content, as a retry would
| Stop a service between saving and publishing | The service is stopped as soon as the test observes the change through its interface, before its relay has run: the relay of scenario runs waits one second between passes to leave that window
| Make two things happen at once | An answer is published at the instant a deadline is due, repeatedly; the invariants are checked each time. The deterministic proof of the same collision is {TI-PLT-003}.
| Act as an administrator | The administration interface, with a test account that has the role
|===

.Tokens of the simulated payment provider
[%unbreakable, cols="3,7,5", options="header"]
|===
| Token | The provider | Used by
| `tok_ok` | Captures | The nominal scenarios
| `tok_declined` | Declines | {SC-ORD-002}
| `tok_slow` | Captures after a delay of a few seconds: shorter than the time limit of the call, hence than the order deadline | A slow payment that still succeeds: {UC-ORD-001}, flow 7a
| `tok_timeout` | Does not answer the charge; cancels when asked | {SC-ORD-005}
| `tok_late_capture` | Captures but does not answer; answers "already captured" to the cancellation | {SC-ORD-006}
| `tok_blackout` | Answers nothing at all: neither the charge, nor a status query, nor a cancellation | {SC-ORD-015}
| `tok_error` | Fails every call | The circuit breaker
| `tok_flaky` | Fails twice, then captures | The retries of the call
| `tok_refund_fails` | Captures at once; every refund fails | {SC-ORD-013}
|===

The simulated provider also has a control interface, absent from any real provider: a test uses it to end a blackout or a refund failure, which is how "the provider works again" is written in a scenario.

.What a scenario observes
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Observation | Through
| The order: status, reason, history | The interface of the order service, as a customer and as an administrator
| What the saga knows: its state, the remembered step, payment and stock | The administrator's view of the order
| Every message, in order, with its envelope | A subscription of the test on each topic
| What the provider was asked: charges, cancellations, refunds, by reference | The record of the simulated provider
| The stock: on hand, reserved, available | The administration interface of the inventory
| The e-mails | The record of the simulated e-mail provider
| Metrics and alerts | The metrics endpoint of each service; the alert rules evaluated on them
|===

A scenario never reads a database: what cannot be observed from outside is not a promise of the platform.

== Scenario sheets

.Strategy question
****
What strategy turns each scenario of the saga specification into a test that anyone can write the same way?
****

Each sheet gives what is arranged, what is done and what must be observed at the end.
Every sheet also asserts the four invariants, which are not repeated in the table: one order per checkout; at most one charge and one refund per order at the provider; available stock never negative and back to its value when the order is not confirmed; at most one e-mail per event.

.Placing an order
[cols="2,5,5,7", options="header"]
|===
| Test | Arrange | Act | Assert at the end
| {TI-ORD-001} | 5 units in stock; `tok_ok` | Place an order for 1 unit | Order `CONFIRMED`; one charge; 4 units on hand, none reserved; the cart is empty; one confirmation e-mail; the messages of document 03-03 in order
| {TI-ORD-002} | `tok_declined` | Place an order | Order `REJECTED`, `PAYMENT_REFUSED`; no capture; the unit is available again; the cart is kept; one rejection e-mail saying nothing was charged
| {TI-ORD-003} | 1 unit in stock | Two customers place an order for it at the same instant | One `CONFIRMED`, one `REJECTED`, `OUT_OF_STOCK`; the provider was called once; no stock is negative
| {TI-ORD-004} | The inventory is stopped | Place an order; let the order deadline pass; start the inventory | Order `REJECTED`, `TIMED_OUT`; the provider was never called; after the inventory returns, nothing stays reserved
| {TI-ORD-005} | `tok_timeout` | Place an order | The payment is canceled at the provider; order `REJECTED`, `TIMED_OUT`; no capture, now or after waiting
| {TI-ORD-006} | `tok_late_capture` | Place an order | Order `CONFIRMED`; exactly one capture; the stock is taken
| {TI-ORD-016} | The payment service is stopped | Place an order; let the order deadline and the review delay pass; start the payment service | While stopped: order `UNDER_REVIEW`. After: the provider was never asked to charge; order `REJECTED`, `TIMED_OUT`; the unit is available
| {TI-ORD-018} | The inventory is stopped after the reservation | Place an order with `tok_ok`; start the inventory before the review delay | Order `CONFIRMED`; it was never `UNDER_REVIEW`
|===

.Technical failures
[cols="2,5,5,7", options="header"]
|===
| Test | Arrange | Act | Assert at the end
| {TI-ORD-007} | `tok_ok` | Stop the order service as soon as the capture is observed on the bus; start it | Order `CONFIRMED`; one capture
| {TI-ORD-008} | `tok_ok` | Run the nominal scenario; republish every observed message, once with its identifier and once as a new message | Exactly the end state of {TI-ORD-001}: one charge, one stock movement, one e-mail
| {TI-ORD-009} | – | For each service of the saga in turn: stop it between its saving and its publication; start it | The message is published after the restart; the order reaches the outcome of {TI-ORD-001}
| {TI-ORD-010} | Two replicas of the order service; the inventory answers at the instant the order deadline is due | Place an order; repeat fifty times | Each order ends `CONFIRMED` or `REJECTED`, `TIMED_OUT`, never both, never neither; the invariants hold each time
|===

.Orders under review
[cols="2,5,5,7", options="header"]
|===
| Test | Arrange | Act | Assert at the end
| {TI-ORD-011} | `tok_ok`; the inventory is stopped after the reservation | Place an order; let the review delay pass; start the inventory | While stopped: order `UNDER_REVIEW`, the alert raised, one e-mail "being checked". After: order `CONFIRMED` with no decision of an administrator
| {TI-ORD-012} | The same, with a reservation lifetime shorter than the outage and the unit bought by another customer meanwhile | Start the inventory | The payment is refunded once; order `REJECTED`, `OUT_OF_STOCK`; an e-mail says the charge was refunded
| {TI-ORD-013} | `tok_refund_fails`; a paid order whose stock is gone | Let the refund fail; then allow refunds and retry as an administrator | First: order `UNDER_REVIEW`, payment still captured, the alert raised. Then: one refund; order `REJECTED`
| {TI-ORD-015} | `tok_blackout` | Place an order; let the review delay pass; reject as an administrator; lift the blackout | While dark: order `UNDER_REVIEW`, payment unknown, the unit still reserved. After: the payment is canceled; order `REJECTED`, `ADMIN_DECISION`; the unit is available
| {TI-ORD-017} | `tok_ok`; the inventory is stopped after the reservation; order `UNDER_REVIEW` | Reject as an administrator; then start the inventory | One refund; order `REJECTED`, `ADMIN_DECISION`; after the inventory returns, the unit is on hand and available: taken, then returned once
|===

.Cancellation
[cols="2,5,5,7", options="header"]
|===
| Test | Arrange | Act | Assert at the end
| {TI-ORD-014} | A confirmed order of 2 units | Cancel it as its customer | Order `CANCELED`; 2 units back on hand; one refund of the full amount; one cancellation e-mail
| {TI-ORD-019} | A confirmed order of 2 units; the inventory is stopped | Cancel; let the review delay pass; retry as an administrator; start the inventory | While stopped: order `UNDER_REVIEW`. After: exactly 2 units back, not 4; one refund; order `CANCELED`
|===

== Systematic sweeps

.Strategy question
****
What strategy finds the failure nobody thought of writing a scenario for?
****

A named scenario proves the case someone imagined.
Sweeps go through all the cases of one kind, mechanically.

.Sweeps
[cols="4,7,5", options="header"]
|===
| Sweep | Method | Depth
| Every cell | A test reads the grids of document 02-03 and checks that each cell has a test, and each test its cell | The saga alone
| Generated sequences | Thousands of random sequences of answers, deadlines and administrator requests are applied to a saga, with the answers the participants could really give; the invariants are checked after each step, and every sequence must end or reach a person | The saga alone
| Duplicate everything | Each message of each scenario is delivered a second time, at once and later | One service; several services
| Stop at every step | For each message handled in the nominal scenario, the handling service is stopped after its transaction and before its acknowledgement, then started | Several services
| Silence at every step | Each participant is silent at each step where the saga waits for it: the table below | Several services
|===

.Silence at every step: who is silent, when, and what must happen
[cols="3,4,9", options="header"]
|===
| Silent | While the saga waits for | Outcome
| Inventory | The reservation | Rejected at the order deadline, never charged ({TI-ORD-004})
| Payment service | The charge | Never charged; rejected when the service returns; under review meanwhile ({TI-ORD-016})
| Payment provider | The charge | Canceled, then rejected; or carried through if captured ({TI-ORD-005}, {TI-ORD-006})
| Payment provider | The cancellation | Under review; settled when the provider returns ({TI-ORD-015})
| Inventory | The confirmation | Confirmed late, or under review and then confirmed ({TI-ORD-018}, {TI-ORD-011})
| Payment provider | The refund | Under review; refunded on a retry ({TI-ORD-013})
| Inventory | The return of a cancellation | Under review; returned once ({TI-ORD-019})
| Order service | Any answer | Answers wait; handled at restart, deadlines first ({TI-ORD-007})
| Notification | – | Orders unaffected; e-mails sent late ({TC-NOT-001})
|===

Every row of the outage table of document 05-02 appears here with a test.

== Resilience tests of the platform

.Strategy question
****
What strategy checks the answers of document 05-02 on the deployed platform, not only in the code?
****

These tests run on `integration`, in an announced window when they disturb the shared database (document 06-05).

[cols="4,6,6", options="header"]
|===
| Test | Method | Passes when
| Loss of an instance | During a steady load, a pod of each service is deleted in turn | No error beyond the requests that pod held; no order lost; the pod is replaced ({NFR-AVAIL-002})
| Loss of a service | Each service is scaled to nothing in turn, for five minutes, under load | The functions that document 05-02 says survive do survive, without error ({NFR-AVAIL-003}); orders settle when the service returns
| Deployment under load | A new version of each service is rolled out while orders are being placed | Not one order fails or is charged twice because of the rollout ({NFR-REL-004})
| Slow neighbor | A simulated catalog answers after the time limit | The call is abandoned in time; the circuit opens, then closes after the trial calls; the cart shows the last known prices
| Failing provider | `tok_error` on a share of the orders | The circuit opens; charges are answered "unknown" at once; orders are rejected uncharged when it closes
| Poison message | A message that breaks its contract is published on each topic | It reaches its dead-letter topic after ten attempts; the alert fires; the other orders are not delayed
| Parked message | A row that the bus refuses is placed in an outbox | It is parked; the alert fires; only the later messages of the same order and topic wait
| Identity provider down | It is scaled to nothing for ten minutes | Browsing works; a session is kept and answered with its code; everything resumes
| Database failover | Triggered in its window | About a minute of clean refusals; no order lost; nothing to do by hand
|===

.Tests of alerts
[%unbreakable, cols="5,10", options="header"]
|===
| Alert | Provoked by
| Orders under review | {TI-ORD-011}, left unresolved
| Dead letter; outbox late or parked | The poison and parked message tests above
| Defensive transition | A `PaymentCaptured` published by the test for a rejected order
| Payment provider unavailable | `tok_error` for longer than the threshold
| Refund failed; e-mail not sent | {TI-ORD-013}; the simulated e-mail provider set to fail
| Error budget, probe | A service scaled to nothing under load; the web gateway scaled to nothing
| Budget, drift | A test budget with a tiny amount in `test`; a label removed by hand from a resource of `test`
|===

Each alert must fire within 5 minutes of its cause and name its runbook ({NFR-OBS-004}).

.In the proof of concept ({ADR-PLT-030})
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | What runs
| Saga scenarios | All of them, in the composition of containers of the pipeline: they need no cloud resource. On the deployed environment: the nominal one, a declined payment, a silent inventory.
| Platform tests | The loss of a service, the poison message, the identity provider down. With one replica, the loss of an instance is the loss of the service.
| Not run | Deployment under load and database failover: they need a second replica and a standby
|===
