== Method

.Strategy question
****
What strategy decides where one microservice ends and the next begins?
****

A service boundary is a business boundary before it is a technical one.
The split follows three tests, applied to the rules of document 01-06 and to the failure analysis of document 01-02.

. *Which rules must be true at the same instant?* Rules that must hold together belong to one context, because only one database transaction can guarantee them. "Available quantity is never negative" ties stock and reservations together; nothing ties stock to a product description.
. *Which data changes at the same rhythm and for the same people?* A product description changes when the administrator edits it. A stock level changes with every order. They are read and written by different flows at different rates.
. *What must keep working when something else is down?* Customers must be able to browse while orders are failing; an e-mail outage must not block a payment.

A boundary is drawn where the three tests agree.
A boundary that only one test supports is a module inside a service, not a service.

== Subdomains

.Strategy question
****
What strategy tells us where to spend design effort, and where to buy or keep things simple?
****

[cols="3,2,10", options="header"]
|===
| Subdomain | Kind | What it means for the design
| Order placement | Core | This is where the store keeps or breaks its promise. It gets the richest domain model, the saga, every failure scenario and the end-to-end tests.
| Inventory | Core | The rule "never oversell" is enforced here under concurrency. Small model, strict invariants.
| Payment | Supporting | The logic is the provider's; ours is to charge once, know the outcome, and give back. A thin model around a port.
| Catalog | Supporting | Mostly data entry and reading. Deliberately simple.
| Cart and favorites | Supporting | Lists of intentions with limits. Deliberately simple.
| Notification | Generic | Sending an e-mail on an event. No business rule beyond "once".
| Identity | Generic | Bought, not built: an identity provider.
|===

== Bounded contexts

.Strategy question
****
What strategy gives each piece of data exactly one owner?
****

Each bounded context is one service with its own database.
No service reads another service's database; whatever it needs, it asks for through an interface or keeps a copy fed by events.

.Bounded contexts and what they own
[cols="3,6,6,5", options="header"]
|===
| Context and service | Responsibility | Owns | Does not own
| Catalog `catalog-service`
| What is sold, at what price, and what availability to show
| Products, categories, prices and their history; a copy of the available quantity of each product
| Stock. The copy is fed by inventory events and is never corrected by hand.

| Inventory `inventory-service`
| What can really be sold
| Stock on hand, reservations, stock movements
| Product descriptions and prices. It knows a product only by its SKU, which it checks with the catalog the first time it sees it.

| Cart `cart-service`
| What a customer intends to buy, and what they like
| Carts, cart lines, favorites
| Prices and availability, which it reads from the catalog each time a cart is shown.

| Order `order-service`
| Turning a cart into a confirmed order, and following it to delivery
| Orders with their frozen lines and prices, the customer's e-mail address as it was at placement, order status history, the order sagas
| Stock and payments. It asks the inventory and the payment services, and records what they answer.

| Payment `payment-service`
| Charging once, knowing the outcome, refunding once
| Payments, attempts, refunds, the link with the payment provider
| Orders. It knows an order only by its identifier and its amount.

| Notification `notification-service`
| Telling the customer what happened
| The record of e-mails sent
| Anything else. It acts on what order events tell it.

| Identity (identity provider)
| Who someone is and which role they hold
| Accounts, credentials, roles
| Everything else. Services see a signed token, never a password.
|===

.Decisions on the boundaries
[cols="5,10", options="header"]
|===
| Question | Decision and reason
| A user service? | *No.* The identity provider owns accounts and roles. A service of our own would only duplicate it. The customer's name and e-mail address come from the token. The e-mail address and the shipping address typed at checkout are copied into the order when it is placed, so that an order can be notified and delivered whoever acts on it later.
| Product service and catalog service? | *One context.* "Product" and "catalog" are the same thing seen by the same person.
| Catalog and inventory together? | *Two contexts.* They fail all three tests together: different invariants, different rhythms, and the catalog must stay readable when the inventory is busy or down.
| Where does the cart live? | *Its own context*, with favorites. A cart is not a draft order: it has no price, reserves nothing and changes constantly. Keeping it apart keeps that churn away from the order database.
| Where does the saga live? | *Inside the order service*, as a module next to the order model. The saga is the life of an order; a separate orchestration service would have to share the order's database or duplicate its state.
| Notification inside the order service? | *Separate.* An e-mail outage must never slow down or fail an order, and the next notification channel should not touch order code.
|===

== Context map

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps one context from being shaped by the internals of another?
****

The map shows who depends on whom and through what.
An arrow points from the one that depends to the one it depends on.

plantuml::diagrams/context-map.puml[format=svg, align=center]

.Relationships
[cols="4,3,5,7", options="header"]
|===
| From → to | Style | Pattern | What crosses the boundary
| Cart → Catalog | Synchronous | Open host service | Current price, status and known available quantity of the products in a cart
| Order → Catalog | Synchronous | Open host service | The same, once, when a checkout is submitted: these prices become the prices of the order
| Order → Cart | Synchronous | Open host service | The lines of the customer's cart, when a checkout is submitted
| Inventory → Catalog | Synchronous | Open host service | Whether a SKU exists, asked once: the first time stock is adjusted for it
| Catalog ← Inventory | Events | Published language | The available quantity of a product, each time it changes
| Order ⇄ Inventory | Commands and events | Customer and supplier | Reserve, confirm, release and return stock; what happened to each request
| Order ⇄ Payment | Commands and events | Customer and supplier | Charge, cancel and refund a payment; what happened to each request
| Cart ← Order | Events | Published language | An order was confirmed: its quantities leave the cart
| Notification ← Order | Events | Published language | An order changed status: tell the customer
| Payment → payment provider | Synchronous | Anti-corruption layer | Charge, cancel, refund, in the provider's own terms, translated at the edge of the service
| Notification → e-mail provider | Synchronous | Anti-corruption layer | An e-mail to deliver
| Every service → identity provider | Token validation | Conformist | The signed token of the actor, following the standard
|===

Three rules follow from the map and are enforced by architecture tests and by contract tests.

. *A synchronous call never changes state in another context.* The four synchronous calls between our services are reads. Every change that crosses a boundary travels as a message, so that it survives the receiver being down.
. *Nobody depends on the order service synchronously.* The core is never on the critical path of another service's request.
. *A context publishes an event only when another context needs it.* The catalog and the cart publish none in this release: nothing consumes what they would say.

.What keeps working when a service is down
[%unbreakable, cols="3,12", options="header"]
|===
| Service down | Effect on the store
| Catalog | The storefront cannot browse; carts show last known prices; checkout is refused; stock cannot be entered for a product the inventory has never seen. The store is effectively closed: the catalog runs with the most replicas.
| Inventory | Browsing and carts work, with the availability last known. An order whose stock is not reserved at the order deadline is rejected. An order already paid waits; past the review delay it is shown under review, and it is confirmed as soon as the inventory is back and answers.
| Cart | Browsing works. No cart, no checkout.
| Order | Browsing, carts and favorites work. No checkout, no order history.
| Payment | Browsing, carts, order history work. A new order waits. If the service is not back at the order deadline the order will never be charged, whenever the service returns; it is rejected as soon as the service confirms that nothing was charged, and is shown under review meanwhile if the outage outlasts the review delay.
| Notification | Everything works. E-mails are sent when it comes back.
| Identity provider | Browsing works. Nobody can sign in; sessions already open keep working until their token expires.
|===
