== Modeling rules

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps every business rule in one place where a unit test can reach it?
****

The domain of each service is plain Java: no framework, no database, no messaging.
A business rule of document 01-06 is a method, or a refusal, of exactly one domain object, and that is where its unit tests live.
These rules apply to every context.

Aggregate:: A cluster of objects that is loaded, changed and saved as a whole, through its root. An aggregate protects its own invariants: no code outside it can put it in an invalid state.
One aggregate per transaction:: A use case changes one aggregate and saves it. The two exceptions are stated where they occur, with their reason.
Reference by identity:: An aggregate refers to another by its identifier, never by an object reference, and never across services by anything else.
Value objects:: `Money`, `Sku`, `Quantity`, `OrderNumber` and the like are immutable, validated when created and compared by value. A price is never a bare number, a SKU never a bare string.
Decisions, not side effects:: A domain object never sends a message or calls a service. It returns what it decided — a new state, the commands to send, the events to publish — and the application layer acts on it.
Versions:: Every aggregate carries a version. Saving an aggregate that changed in the meantime fails; the use case is run again on the fresh state — a few times at once, then by a new delivery of the message (document 03-06).
Amounts:: Decimal numbers with two decimals in EUR. Never floating-point.
Time:: The current time is given to the domain, never read by it, so that deadlines and expiries are testable.

Two views are given for each context: the *class diagram* of the domain objects, and the *entity-relationship diagram* of the tables that the PostgreSQL adapter uses.
They are deliberately not the same thing: tables serve the adapter and may change without touching the domain.

== Order

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps the business state of an order apart from the mechanics that drive it?
****

The context has two kinds of aggregate.
`Order` is the business object: what was bought, for how much, and where it stands.
A saga — `PlaceOrderSaga`, `CancelOrderSaga` — is the process: which step is in progress, which deadlines run, what failed.
The saga decides; the order records.

A saga holds more than a state.
It keeps what the payment service and the inventory last told it, the step it was in when it was suspended, and a rejection that is decided but must wait for the money.
These fields are what lets it give an answer to every event in every state; document 02-03 specifies them one by one.

plantuml::diagrams/class-order.puml[format=svg, align=center]

.Invariants of the order context
[cols="5,4,6", options="header"]
|===
| Invariant | Guarded by | Rules
| An order has at least one line, an address and a total equal to the sum of its lines | `Order.place` | {FR-ORD-001} {FR-ORD-004}
| Lines, prices and address never change after placement | `Order` has no method to change them | {FR-ORD-002} {FR-ORD-011}
| Status changes follow the allowed transitions; a final status never changes | Every method of `Order` checks the current status | {FR-ORD-010}
| A rejection carries exactly one reason | `Order.reject` | {FR-ORD-008}
| Confirmation needs a captured payment and a confirmed reservation | `PlaceOrderSaga` reaches that decision only through those two events | {FR-ORD-007}
| An order is rejected only once it holds no payment | `PlaceOrderSaga` rejects only from states where the payment is refused, canceled, refunded or never requested | {FR-ORD-008} {FR-PAY-005}
| Deadlines lead to a decision | `onOrderDeadline`, `onReviewDeadline` of the sagas | {FR-ORD-015} {FR-ORD-016}
| A late answer is never dropped: a suspended saga handles it as in the step it remembers | `on(event)` of the sagas | {FR-ORD-016} {NFR-REL-002}
| An administrator's decision obeys the rules of the automatic flow | `retry`, `confirm`, `reject` of the sagas | {FR-ORD-018}
|===

A saga and its order are saved in the same transaction, together with the messages the decision produces.
This is the first exception to "one aggregate per transaction", and a safe one: both live in the same service and the same database, and the saga exists only to move that one order.

plantuml::diagrams/er-order.puml[format=svg, align=center]

Three constraints of the database back the domain:

* `checkout_id` is unique: submitting the same checkout twice cannot create two orders ({FR-ORD-009});
* at most one row per customer has the status `PENDING` ({FR-ORD-017});
* `outbox` and `inbox` are written in the same transaction as the order, which is what makes messaging reliable (document 03-06).

Two columns deserve a word.
`customer_email` is copied from the customer's token when the order is placed: an e-mail about the order can then be sent whoever triggers it, an administrator shipping it for instance.
`payment_token` holds the token of the payment provider between the checkout and the charge request, and is erased in the transaction that writes that request; document 03-06 gives the complete rule.

== Inventory

.Strategy question
****
What strategy guarantees "never oversell" when several orders reserve the same product at the same instant?
****

`StockItem` holds the two numbers that matter for one SKU, on hand and reserved, and refuses any operation that would break their relation.
`Reservation` records what is held for one order, until when, and what became of it.

plantuml::diagrams/class-inventory.puml[format=svg, align=center]

.Invariants of the inventory context
[cols="5,4,6", options="header"]
|===
| Invariant | Guarded by | Rules
| On hand and reserved are never negative, and reserved never exceeds on hand | Every method of `StockItem` | {FR-INV-001} {FR-INV-002}
| A reservation covers all lines or none | `ReservationPolicy.reserveAllOrNothing` | {FR-INV-003}
| A request about a reservation is answered from its status; the stock changes only when that status shows the request is not served yet | Every method of `Reservation` | {FR-INV-005} {NFR-REL-002}
| The units of an order are returned at most once | `Reservation.giveBack`: `CONFIRMED` becomes `RETURNED` | {FR-ORD-012}
| A reservation past its lifetime expires and frees its quantities | `Reservation.expire`, called by a scheduled task | {FR-INV-004}
| An adjustment never brings on hand below reserved | `StockItem.adjust` | {FR-INV-006}
| Every change of stock on hand leaves a movement | `StockItem` returns the movement with each change | {FR-INV-008}
|===

Reserving an order touches one `StockItem` per line and creates one `Reservation`, in one transaction.
This is the second exception to "one aggregate per transaction": all-or-nothing across lines is the business rule itself.
Stock rows are locked in SKU order so that two orders reserving the same products can never deadlock.

plantuml::diagrams/er-inventory.puml[format=svg, align=center]

The three `check` constraints repeat the invariants of `StockItem` in the database: even a defect in the code cannot store an oversold stock.
`order_id` is unique in `reservation`: every request about the stock of an order finds the one reservation of that order and is answered from its status.
A reservation that could not be made is recorded too, as `REJECTED`, so that a repeated request gets the same answer.
`saga_id` says which saga asked for it.

== Payment

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes "charged at most once" a property of the model rather than a hope?
****

There is one `Payment` per order, found by the order identifier.
Every request about an order's payment — charge, cancel, refund — starts by loading that one object, which answers from its recorded state before anything is asked of the provider.

plantuml::diagrams/class-payment.puml[format=svg, align=center]

.Invariants of the payment context
[cols="5,4,6", options="header"]
|===
| Invariant | Guarded by | Rules
| One payment per order | Unique order identifier; a second charge request is answered from the status of that payment | {FR-PAY-001}
| The amount is the order total, in EUR | `Payment` is created with the amount and never changes it | {FR-PAY-002}
| A payment is refunded only if captured, and only once | `Payment.canBeRefunded`, `recordRefund` | {FR-PAY-006}
| A canceled payment can no longer be captured | `Payment.recordCancellation`; the provider is called with the same reference | {FR-PAY-005}
| No card data is held | `Payment` has no field for it; the token is passed to the provider and never written by the payment service | {FR-PAY-003}
|===

The payment provider is reached through the `PaymentProvider` port.
The simulated provider of this release and a real one later are two adapters of the same port; the domain cannot tell them apart.

== Catalog, cart and notification

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps the simple contexts simple?
****

These three contexts hold little logic, and their models say so.
They are also the ones whose persistence is interchangeable (catalog and cart), which only works because nothing in their domain depends on how data is stored.

plantuml::diagrams/class-catalog.puml[format=svg, align=center]

`Product` guards the rules of the catalog: a unique and permanent SKU, a price within range, publication only when complete, retirement for good ({FR-CAT-001} to {FR-CAT-005}, {FR-CAT-009}).
`Availability` is not part of the product: it is a read model, fed by inventory events, that keeps the last quantity announced for each SKU.
Each announcement carries a sequence number; an announcement older than the one already applied is ignored, so that events arriving out of order cannot bring an old quantity back ({FR-CAT-008}, {FR-INV-007}).

plantuml::diagrams/class-cart.puml[format=svg, align=center]

`Cart` stores only what the customer chose: a SKU, a quantity, and the price seen when the line was added.
Totals, the counter and the flags are computed each time the cart is read, from the current catalog data, into a `CartView` ({FR-CART-005}, {FR-CART-006}, {FR-CART-010}).
That is why a price change needs no update of any cart.

A cart also remembers the orders whose quantities it already gave up.
Removing them subtracts, so doing it twice would be wrong: `removeOrdered` does nothing for an order it has already applied ({FR-CART-007}).
This record lives inside the cart itself, which keeps it atomic in each of the three stores.

The notification context has no domain model worth a diagram: one record per event received, with the outcome of the sending.
The record is written before the e-mail provider is called and completed afterwards, and the identifier of the event is given to the provider as an idempotency key, so that an event produces at most one e-mail even when the service stops between the two ({FR-NOT-002}; document 03-06).

plantuml::diagrams/er-catalog-cart-notification.puml[format=svg, align=center]

The tables above are those of the PostgreSQL adapter.
For the catalog and the cart, the document-database and file adapters store the same aggregates as documents — one document per product, per cart, per list of favorites — as specified in document 04-04.

== Shared vocabulary of types

.Strategy question
****
What strategy lets services exchange data without sharing code?
****

Services share no domain class.
What they share is the *meaning* of a few types, fixed here and carried by the message and API contracts.

[%unbreakable, cols="3,5,7", options="header"]
|===
| Type | Form | Rule
| `Sku` | Text, 3 to 32 characters, upper-case letters, digits and hyphens | Assigned by the administrator, permanent, never reused
| `Money` | An exact amount with two decimals, and a currency. A decimal number in the domain and in the REST interfaces; a whole number of cents in messages (document 03-04) | The currency is always EUR in this release. Never a floating-point number.
| `Quantity` | Whole number, 1 or more | What a customer buys. Zero is not a quantity: a line with nothing is removed
| `StockLevel` | Whole number, 0 or more | What the inventory counts: on hand, reserved, available. Zero is a level: out of stock
| `PaymentToken` | Opaque text issued by the payment provider | Single use. Handled as a secret: never logged, kept only until the charge is requested
| `OrderId`, `SagaId`, `PaymentId`, `ReservationId` | Universally unique identifier | Generated by the service that creates the object; time-ordered
| `OrderNumber` | Text shown to the customer | Unique, readable, gives no hint of the number of orders
| `CustomerId` | The subject of the identity token | Never an e-mail address
| `CheckoutId` | Universally unique identifier | Generated by the storefront when the checkout review is shown
|===
