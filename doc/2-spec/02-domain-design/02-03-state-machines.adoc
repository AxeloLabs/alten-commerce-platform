== Why two machines for one order

.Strategy question
****
What strategy shows the customer a few simple statuses while the system juggles a dozen technical steps?
****

An order has two lives.
Its *business status* is what the customer, the administrator and every other document talk about: pending, confirmed, rejected, under review, and so on.
Its *saga state* is where the processing stands: which request was sent, which answer is awaited, which deadline is running.

Mixing them would force a choice between a status list nobody outside the team understands, and a processing model too coarse to recover from a crash.
They are therefore two machines: the saga decides, and a transition of the saga may — or may not — move the order.

No state machine engine is used.
Each machine is a plain domain class holding its state and one method per event.
*Every* pair of state and event has a defined outcome.
This document gives it as a grid for each saga, and as a table of answers for each participant; each cell of each grid is a unit test.

| `UNDER_REVIEW` | `REJECTED` | The place-order saga is compensated: after a late answer, or on an administrator's request | The order holds no payment
| `CONFIRMED` | `SHIPPED` | An administrator marks it shipped | –
| `SHIPPED` | `DELIVERED` | An administrator marks it delivered | –
| `CONFIRMED` | `CANCELING` | The customer cancels | –
| `CANCELING` | `CANCELED` | The cancel-order saga completes | Stock returned and payment refunded
| `CANCELING` | `UNDER_REVIEW` | The refund keeps failing, or the cancellation is not finished when the review delay expires | –
| `UNDER_REVIEW` | `CANCELED` | The cancel-order saga completes: the answer that was missing arrived, or a retry succeeded | The order was being canceled; stock returned and payment refunded
|===

Any other pair is refused.
In particular nothing leaves `REJECTED`, `DELIVERED` or `CANCELED`, whatever message or request arrives.

An order becomes `UNDER_REVIEW` the first time its saga is suspended.
It keeps that status while the saga works again, and leaves it only when the saga ends.

== Place-order saga

.Strategy question
****
What strategy lets an order placement stop at any instant, resume exactly where it was, and have an answer ready for every event in every state?
****

The saga state is saved, in the same transaction as the request it sends, each time it changes.
After a crash the service reads the state and knows what was last asked and what is awaited; nothing has to be remembered in memory.

.What the saga remembers
[cols="4,8,6", options="header"]
|===
| Field | Content | Used for
| State | One of the eight states | Choosing the column of the grid
| Remembered step | The working state the saga was in when it was suspended; kept until the saga ends | Handling a late answer; knowing what "retry" means
| Known payment | What the saga last asked of the payment service or was told by it: not requested, requested, unknown, captured, refund requested, refused, canceled, refunded. Once a refund is requested, the payment is never known as captured again. | Rule 5; guards of the administrator's requests; what the administrator is shown
| Known stock | What the saga last asked of the inventory or was told by it: requested, held, rejected, confirmed, lost, released, return requested, returned | Rule 4; what the administrator is shown
| Decided rejection | Empty, or the reason of a rejection that is decided but must wait until no payment is held: `OUT_OF_STOCK` or `ADMIN_DECISION` | Transitions P08 to P10 and P14
| Order deadline, review deadline | Two instants; either may be empty | The deadlines below
| Last failure | What the last failed answer said | The history shown to the administrator
| Version | A counter checked at each save | Two events handled at once (document 03-06)
|===

Five rules hold in every state.
They are what makes the grid below short enough to read.

. *Facts are never dropped.* Whatever the state, an answer updates what the saga knows about the payment and about the stock. What differs from state to state is only what the saga does next.
. *A suspended saga stops asking; it does not stop listening.* In `SUSPENDED` the saga sends nothing by itself and has no deadline. But the request it was waiting on is still in the subscription of the service that was silent, and that service executes it when it comes back. The saga handles that answer exactly as it would have in the remembered step: it follows what really happened, with or without an administrator.
. *A rejection waits for the money.* The saga rejects the order only in a transition where it has just learned that no payment is held: never requested, refused, canceled or refunded. A rejection decided earlier — by an administrator, or because the stock is lost — is recorded as _decided_ and carried out by that transition ({FR-ORD-008}, {FR-ORD-018}).
. *Stock is undone after the money, according to what the inventory said.* "Undoes the stock" means: sends `ReturnStock` if the stock is known as confirmed, `ReleaseReservation` if it is known as held or requested, nothing otherwise. A release that finds the stock already taken says so, and the saga then sends the return (P21).
. *An order is confirmed only on money that is there to stay.* The saga sends `ConfirmReservation`, and confirms the order, only while it knows the payment as captured and has never asked for its refund. A refund that was requested cannot be recalled: the request waits in the subscription of the payment service and may be executed at any time, even days later. From then on the saga can only finish the rejection ({FR-ORD-007}).

[horizontal]
P02 … P23:: The transition of that number, in the table below.
–:: No effect. The fact is recorded in the history of the saga; nothing is sent and the state does not change. This covers duplicates, answers that no longer matter, and events that should never occur in that state; the last are also counted and logged as unexpected.
step:: Handled as in the remembered step (rule 2): as the column CAN, CNF or REF says.
refused:: The request is refused, and the answer says why: the order is not under review, or it is, but a step asked earlier is still in progress.
(a):: The fact matters later: P14 returns a stock known as confirmed, and releases nothing for a stock known as lost.
(b):: In RES and CHG the order deadline is always due before the review deadline and is handled first; it takes the saga out of these states.

.What an administrator's decision does, according to the remembered step
[%unbreakable, cols="4,5,5,5", options="header"]
|===
| Remembered step | Retry (P17) | Confirm (P18) | Reject (P19)
| `CANCELING_PAYMENT`: nobody knows whether the payment was captured | `CancelPayment` again | Refused: the payment is not known as captured | `CancelPayment`; the rejection is decided
| `CONFIRMING_STOCK`: paid, stock not confirmed | `ConfirmReservation` again | The same as a retry | `RefundPayment`; the rejection is decided
| `REFUNDING_PAYMENT`: paid, a refund was requested | `RefundPayment` again; the reason already decided is kept | Refused: a refund that was requested cannot be recalled (rule 5) | `RefundPayment` again; the reason becomes `ADMIN_DECISION`
|===

The three requests of an administrator are addressed to the order and apply to its suspended saga.
An order never has two sagas at work: a cancellation can start only on a confirmed order, whose placement saga is completed.
An administrator who acts a moment after the missing answer arrived is refused, because the order is no longer under review; nothing is done twice.
A request is also refused while the saga is at work again after an earlier request: the answer says that a step is in progress, and the saga is suspended again at its new review deadline at the latest.

.Deadlines of the place-order saga
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| Order deadline | Set once, when the saga starts: 2 minutes. It matters in `RESERVING_STOCK` and `CHARGING_PAYMENT` only and is never set again ({FR-ORD-015}).
| Review deadline | Set when the saga starts: 5 minutes ({FR-ORD-016}). Set again, to the current instant plus the review delay, each time the saga leaves `SUSPENDED` for a working state: after a request of an administrator, or after a late answer that calls for one more step.
| Both are due at once | This happens when the order service was stopped. The order deadline is handled first, then the review deadline, against the state the first one produced.
| A deadline that is handled | It is emptied, whatever the cell of the grid says, including when it has no effect. The task looks only for deadlines that are set and passed: none is handled twice.
| `SUSPENDED`, `COMPLETED`, `COMPENSATED` | No deadline runs.
|===

A saga can be suspended only from `CANCELING_PAYMENT`, `CONFIRMING_STOCK` or `REFUNDING_PAYMENT`: the order deadline always takes it out of the first two states before the review deadline is due.
The remembered step is therefore one of these three, and "retry" never means reserving or charging again.

Transitions P22 and P23 are defensive: neither can occur unless a defect exists somewhere.
The status machine of a payment makes a capture impossible once its order is rejected: an order is rejected only on a payment that is refused, canceled or refunded, and none of the three can be captured afterwards — not even by a charge request that comes back days later (section "Payment" below).
If the event arrives all the same, the saga stays `COMPENSATED`, because nothing leaves `REJECTED`; it records the fact, raises a critical alert and asks for the refund.
`PaymentRefunded` closes the alert.
`PaymentRefundFailed` leaves it open, and the on-call engineer follows the runbook of document 06-07: the order is listed among the anomalies until the money is returned.

P23 is its mirror.
Rule 5 makes it impossible for the money of an order to leave while the saga confirms it or after it is confirmed: the saga never confirms once it has asked for a refund, and a cancellation asks for its refund under its own saga identifier, so that answer never reaches the placement saga.
If the payment service says all the same that the payment is gone, a saga that is still confirming stops and waits for a person, who can only reject the order: a retry or a confirmation is refused by rule 5, and a rejection ends through P08 or P09.
A completed saga stays completed, and the order is listed among the anomalies.

The review deadline of a cancellation follows the rules of a placement: set at C01, set again each time the saga leaves `SUSPENDED` for a working state — by C06, or by a late `StockReturned` — and emptied when it is handled.

The stock is returned before the refund is asked, so that a refund in difficulty never keeps units out of sale.
A retry of the return cannot count the stock twice: the inventory returns the units of a reservation once, and answers a repeated request from what it recorded (section "Reservation and product").

An administrator can only retry a cancellation.
Confirming or rejecting it is refused: the customer decided, and the only way out is to finish what they asked.

== Payment

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes sure a payment is never in two states at once, and that every request about it has one answer whatever was done before?
****

plantuml::diagrams/payment-status.puml[format=svg, align=center]

.Transitions of the payment status
[%unbreakable, cols="3,3,9", options="header"]
|===
| From | To | When
| – | `PENDING` | A charge is requested, in time, for an order that has no payment yet
| – | `CANCELED` | A charge is requested too late; or a cancellation or a refund is requested for an order that has no payment
| `PENDING` | `CAPTURED` | The provider accepts
| `PENDING` | `REFUSED` | The provider declines
| `PENDING` | `UNKNOWN` | The provider does not answer after the retries, or keeps failing
| `PENDING`, `UNKNOWN` | `CANCELED` | A cancellation is requested and the provider accepts it
| `PENDING`, `UNKNOWN` | `CAPTURED` | The provider answers "already captured" to a cancellation
| `CAPTURED` | `REFUNDED` | A refund is requested and the provider accepts it
|===

`REFUSED`, `CANCELED` and `REFUNDED` are final: a payment in one of them is never captured afterwards.
A refund that fails leaves the payment `CAPTURED`: the status always says where the money is.

| No payment; the instant given by the request has passed
| The payment is saved as `CANCELED`. The provider is not called: nothing is charged.
| `PaymentCanceled`

| `PENDING`: an earlier handling was interrupted
| In time: the provider is asked again, with the same reference, and answers what it did the first time. Too late: the payment is saved as `UNKNOWN` and the provider is not called.
| As in the first row, or `PaymentOutcomeUnknown`

| `UNKNOWN`
| Nothing. A charge whose outcome is unknown is never attempted again: only a cancellation can settle it.
| `PaymentOutcomeUnknown`

| `CAPTURED`, `REFUSED`, `CANCELED`, `REFUNDED`
| Nothing.
| The event of that status

.4+| `CancelPayment`
| No payment
| The payment is saved as `CANCELED`, so that a charge request arriving later is not executed.
| `PaymentCanceled`

| `PENDING`, `UNKNOWN`
| The provider is asked to cancel, with the same reference. Canceled: `CANCELED`. "Already captured": `CAPTURED`. No answer after the retries: `UNKNOWN`.
| `PaymentCanceled`, `PaymentCaptured` or `PaymentOutcomeUnknown`

| `CAPTURED`
| Nothing. A captured payment is never canceled ({FR-PAY-005}).

| `REFUNDED`, `REFUSED`, `CANCELED`

| The event of that status

| No payment
| The payment is saved as `CANCELED`.
| `PaymentCanceled`

| `PENDING`, `UNKNOWN`

| `PaymentOutcomeUnknown`
|===

"The event of that status" is `PaymentCaptured`, `PaymentRefused`, `PaymentCanceled` or `PaymentRefunded`.

One rule stands behind every row: *the payment service answers from the current status of the payment*.
If the status shows that the request is served, the answer is published again and the provider is not called.
If it shows that it is not — never tried, interrupted, or failed the last time — the action is performed, with the same provider reference, so that the provider itself cannot do it twice ({FR-PAY-001}, {FR-PAY-006}).
This is what makes an administrator's retry useful: a refund that failed is really attempted again, and a refund that succeeded is not.

The inventory follows the same rule as the payment service: it answers from the current status of the reservation, and changes the stock only when that status shows the request is not served yet.
A reservation that is `HELD` and whose expiry instant has passed is set to `EXPIRED` by a scheduled task of the inventory; its quantities become available again ({FR-INV-004}).
Every row that changes an available quantity also publishes `StockAvailabilityChanged` ({FR-INV-007}).
