== What persistence must guarantee

.Strategy question
****
What strategy states what the business needs from storage before any database is named?
****

The domain does not know where it is stored.
What it needs from storage is a short list of guarantees, and a store qualifies for a service only if it can give all of those the service relies on.

.Guarantees asked of a store
[cols="5,6,4", options="header"]
|===
| Guarantee | Why | Needed by
| An aggregate is saved whole or not at all | An order without its lines, a reservation without its stock change, must not exist | Every service
| A save fails if the aggregate changed since it was read | Two requests about one thing never overwrite each other ({FR-CAT-009}, document 03-06) | Every service
| A business key is unique | One order per checkout, one pending order per customer, one payment and one reservation per order, one SKU per product | Every service, each for its keys
| An aggregate, the record of the message handled and the messages to send are saved together | The outbox and the inbox ({NFR-REL-001}, {NFR-REL-002}) | Order, inventory, payment
| Several aggregates are changed together | Reserving every line of an order or none ({FR-INV-003}); a saga with its order | Inventory, order
| Rows can be claimed by one worker and skipped by the others | The relay, the deadline task, the expiry task on several replicas | Order, inventory, payment
| Lists are filtered, sorted and paged | Catalog browsing, order history, stock review | Catalog, order, inventory
|===

The last four rows are what separates the stores.
A relational database gives all seven.
A document database gives the first three, and the others only within limits; a file gives the first three on a single replica.
This is the reason, and the only reason, why the swap of store is offered on two services and not on six ({ADR-PLT-013}).

== The ports

.Strategy question
****
What strategy hides the store without hiding what the application needs from it?
****

A port of persistence is named after a need of the application, never after a technology.
There are three kinds.

.Kinds of persistence port
[%unbreakable, cols="3,7,6", options="header"]
|===
| Kind | Contract | Examples
| Store of an aggregate | Load by identifier or business key; nothing else. Returns the aggregate with its version. | `OrderStore`, `ReservationStore`, `ProductStore`, `CartStore`
| Unit of work | "Save these aggregates at these versions, record this incoming message, add these outgoing messages" — all or nothing. The one port whose contract includes atomicity. | One per service of a saga
| Queries | Read-only lists and views, returned as read models, never as aggregates. Filters, sort and paging are parameters of the port. | `CatalogQueries`, `OrderHistory`, `StockLevels`
|===

Reads and writes go through different ports on purpose.
A list of two hundred products does not need two hundred aggregates with their invariants; and a store is free to answer a query its own way — a join, an index, a scan — without the domain noticing.

The catalog and the cart publish nothing, so they need no unit of work: saving one aggregate is one atomic write in each of the three stores.
The record of the messages they consume lives inside what they save (document 03-06).

== Which store for which service

.Strategy question
****
What strategy decides where the freedom to swap is worth its price?
****

.Stores by service
[%unbreakable, cols="4,3,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| Service | PostgreSQL | Firestore | File
| Catalog | Yes: the default | Yes | Yes
| Cart | Yes: the default | Yes | Yes
| Order | Yes: the only one | – | –
| Inventory | Yes: the only one | – | –
| Payment | Yes: the only one | – | –
| Notification | Yes: the only one | – | –
| Web gateway, identity provider | Yes: sessions, accounts | – | –
|===

plantuml::diagrams/persistence-ports.puml[format=svg, align=center, pdfwidth=75%]

.Rules of the swap
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| Selection | One configuration property names the store of a service. Exactly one adapter is active; the others are not even instantiated.
| No mixing | A service uses one store for everything it persists. Two stores in one service would need a transaction across them, which nothing provides.
| Proof | One suite of contract tests states the behavior of each port once and runs against the three adapters ({NFR-MAINT-004}). An adapter that passes it can replace another; nothing else is promised.
| File store | For a workstation, a demonstration and tests. One replica only. The service refuses to start with it in the production environment.
| Migration between stores | Not provided. Changing the store of a running environment is a data migration to plan, not a setting to flip.
|===

.What the contract tests assert, for each adapter
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Port | Assertions
| Stores | What is saved is read back equal. A save on an outdated version fails and changes nothing. A second aggregate with the same key is refused. An unknown key gives "absent", not an error.
| `CatalogQueries` | Each filter alone and combined: keyword, category, price range, availability, status. Each sort, with a stable order between equal values. Paging: sizes, last page, page beyond the end ({FR-CAT-007}, {FR-CAT-010}, {FR-CAT-011}).
| `AvailabilityStore` | A quantity with an older sequence number never replaces a newer one ({FR-CAT-008}).
| `CartStore` | Removing the quantities of an order already applied changes nothing ({FR-CART-007}). Limits of the cart hold after a reload.
| Concurrency | Two writers on one aggregate: exactly one succeeds.
|===

== PostgreSQL adapter

.Strategy question
****
What strategy uses a relational database fully without letting it shape the domain?
****

PostgreSQL {v-postgresql} is the store of every service by default, and the only one for four of them ({ADR-PLT-012}).
Its tables are drawn in document 02-02.

.Choices of the PostgreSQL adapter
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Choice
| Access | Spring Data JPA on Hibernate {v-hibernate}. Entities are classes of the adapter, mapped to and from the domain by a mapper; the domain is never annotated ({AR-PLT-010}).
| Transactions | Declared in the adapter only ({AR-PLT-012}), one per unit of work. Read-only queries run outside a write transaction.
| Versions | A version column on every aggregate table, checked at each update. A conflict is reported to the application as "changed since read", not as a database error.
| Unique keys | Declared as constraints, including the partial one that allows a single pending order per customer ({FR-ORD-017}). A violation is reported as "already exists".
| Stock rows | Locked for update in SKU order before a reservation changes them, so that two orders on the same products cannot deadlock.
| Claiming rows | The relay and the scheduled tasks select rows with a lock that other transactions skip, in plain SQL: this is PostgreSQL behavior, not something the persistence framework abstracts.
| Lazy loading | An aggregate is loaded whole, in one query or a fixed number of them. No query is triggered outside the adapter: the session does not stay open during the rendering of a response.
| Free-form data | The shipping address and message attributes are stored as binary JSON columns; they are never searched.
| Keyword search | The full-text search of PostgreSQL in its simplest configuration, on the product name, matching the start of a word and ignoring case and accents.
|===

.Connections
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Choice
| Path | Private address of the Cloud SQL instance, through the Cloud SQL connector for Java. No database port is open to the outside.
| Identity | Each service signs in to its own database with its own Google identity. No database password exists for a service ({NFR-SEC-004}).
| Rights | The identity that runs a service can read and write rows, not change the schema. Migrations run under a separate identity.
| Pool | One pool per replica, small and fixed. With virtual threads the pool is what bounds the concurrency of a service on its database ({ADR-PLT-003}).
| Budget | The pools of all replicas of all services that share an instance, plus those of the identity provider and of the migrations, must stay under 80 % of the connections the instance accepts. The figures are set with the sizing, in document 06-03.
|===

.Indexes the design relies on
[cols="4,6,5", options="header"]
|===
| Table | Index | Serves
| `outbox` | Unpublished rows by destination, ordering key and creation time | The relay and its eligibility rule (document 03-06)
| `inbox` | Reception time | The cleaning task
| `order_saga` | Sagas with an order deadline; sagas with a review deadline | The deadline task
| `orders` | Customer and placement time; status and placement time | Order history; search by administrators; orders under review
| `orders` | Unique, on the customer, for pending orders only | {FR-ORD-017}
| `reservation` | Expiry time, for held reservations only | The expiry task
| `product` | Status and category; price; the search vector of the name | Catalog browsing
| `stock_movement` | SKU and time | Stock history
|===

== Firestore adapter

.Strategy question
****
What strategy fits aggregates into a document database, and says honestly what does not fit?
****

Firestore is used in its native mode, through the client library of Google Cloud and its blocking interface ({ADR-PLT-003}).
The Spring Data module for Firestore is reactive only and is not used.

.Document model
[cols="4,5,7", options="header"]
|===
| Collection | Document | Content
| `products` | One per SKU | The product, its version, the price history as an array, and the availability: quantity and sequence number
| `carts` | One per customer | The lines, the version, the orders already applied
| `favorites` | One per customer | The SKUs marked, with their dates
|===

One aggregate is one document, so that saving it is one atomic write.
The availability is kept inside the product document, which lets a list be filtered on it; it is updated field by field, without touching the version that protects the edits of an administrator.

.Choices of the Firestore adapter
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Choice
| Concurrency | A write is conditional on the version read; a Firestore transaction reads, checks and writes. The availability update is conditional on its sequence number.
| Unique keys | The key is the document identifier: a SKU, a customer. Creating an existing document fails.
| Keyword search | Firestore has no full-text search. Each product stores the prefixes of the words of its name, normalized; a keyword matches when it is one of them. The cost is a larger document, bounded by the maximum length of a name.
| Queries | Every supported combination of filter and sort has a composite index, declared with the infrastructure. A combination that is not in the contract tests is not supported.
| Counting | The total of a filtered list is obtained by a count query; it is read after the page and may differ by one when the catalog changes in between. The contract tests allow for it.
|===

== File adapter

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes the simplest possible store honest about its limits?
****

The file adapter keeps one JSON file per aggregate, in one directory per kind: a product, a cart, a list of favorites.

.Choices of the file adapter
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Choice
| Atomic write | The new content is written to a temporary file, flushed, and renamed over the old one. A crash leaves the old version or the new one, never a mixture.
| Concurrency | One lock per file inside the process, and the version stored in the file. Nothing protects two processes: hence one replica.
| Queries | Files are read into memory at startup and kept in step with writes. Filters and sorts are applied in memory.
| Format | The same field names as the REST interface, readable and editable by hand. That is its value on a workstation.
| Limits | No second replica, no production, no large catalog. Stated at startup in the log, and enforced for production.
|===

.The three stores side by side
[cols="4,4,4,4", options="header"]
|===
| Aspect | PostgreSQL | Firestore | File
| Atomic save of one aggregate | Transaction | Single document write | Rename of a file
| Several aggregates together | Yes | Within transaction limits; not used | No
| Outbox and inbox with the aggregate | Yes | Not offered | No
| Unique keys | Constraints | Document identifiers | File names
| Keyword search | Full-text search | Stored prefixes | Scan in memory
| Replicas of the service | Any number | Any number | One
| Backup | Managed, with recovery to a point in time | Managed exports | Copy of a directory
|===

== Schema migrations

.Strategy question
****
What strategy changes a schema while the previous version of the service is still running?
****

Each service owns its schema and its migrations ({ADR-PLT-014}).

.Rules for migrations
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| Tool and form | Flyway {v-flyway}, plain SQL files in the module of the service, numbered and never edited once merged.
| When | A dedicated step of the deployment applies them before the new version starts, under an identity that may change the schema. A service never migrates at startup: several replicas would race.
| Compatibility | A migration must suit both the version that is running and the one that arrives ({NFR-REL-004}). A change that breaks the old version is split: add in one release, use in the next, remove in a third.
| Forbidden in one step | Renaming or dropping a column in use; adding a mandatory column without a default; rewriting a large table under a lock.
| Data | Reference data that the code relies on — the list of categories — is a migration. Test and demonstration data is not: it is loaded by a separate, repeatable script.
| Firestore and files | No schema to migrate. A document carries the version of its format, and the adapter upgrades an old format when it reads it.
| Verification | The pipeline applies every migration to an empty PostgreSQL and to a copy of the previous schema, then runs the persistence tests.
|===

== H2 and the tests of persistence

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps tests fast, as the brief requires, without trusting a database that is not the one in production?
****

H2 {v-h2} is imposed for speed, and it is not PostgreSQL ({ADR-PLT-015}).
Each kind of test therefore has one database, chosen by what the test proves.

.Which database for which test
[cols="5,4,7", options="header"]
|===
| Test | Database | Reason
| Domain rules, state machines, sagas | None | The domain does not know storage; these are the fastest and the most numerous tests
| Mapping between entities and domain; simple queries of a store | H2 in PostgreSQL mode, in memory | Milliseconds to start; no PostgreSQL-specific behavior involved
| Unique constraints, partial index, version conflicts | PostgreSQL in a container | Constraint behavior differs between engines
| Outbox relay, claiming rows, stock locking, deadline task | PostgreSQL in a container | Depends on PostgreSQL locking; H2 would pass and production would fail
| Migrations | PostgreSQL in a container | They are PostgreSQL SQL
| Keyword search | PostgreSQL in a container | Uses PostgreSQL full-text search
| Contract tests of the three adapters | PostgreSQL in a container, Firestore emulator, temporary directory | The point is to compare real stores
|===

One container of PostgreSQL is started once per build of a module and shared by its tests, each in its own schema.
The detail of test categories and their execution is in document 07-01.

== The life of data

.Strategy question
****
What strategy says how long each piece of data lives, and who may see it?
****

.Retention
[cols="4,4,8", options="header"]
|===
| Data | Kept | Note
| Orders, their lines and history | Not deleted in this release | A legal retention period is a decision of the business, to take before a real opening
| Payments and provider attempts | As long as their order | No card data, by construction ({FR-PAY-003})
| Stock movements | Not deleted | The audit trail of the stock ({FR-INV-008})
| Outbox rows | 7 days after publication | Payload erased at publication when it carries a secret (document 03-06)
| Inbox rows | 30 days | Longer than any replay window
| Sessions of the web gateway | Until sign-out or expiry | Detail in document 05-04
| Carts and favorites | Until the customer changes them | –
|===

.Personal data
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Where | What
| Identity provider | The account: e-mail address, name, credentials. The only place credentials exist ({FR-IAM-006}).
| Order database | The customer identifier, the e-mail address and the shipping address, as they were when the order was placed.
| Notification database | The recipient of each e-mail sent.
| Everywhere else | The customer identifier only, which is not an e-mail address.
|===

Personal data is encrypted at rest with the rest of each database, never written to a log ({NFR-SEC-007}), and never copied from production to another environment.
Erasing or anonymizing the data of a customer who asks for it is not part of this release; the table above is the list of places such a procedure would have to cover.

Backups, recovery to a point in time and the recovery objectives of {NFR-AVAIL-004} are specified with the platform, in document 06-02.
