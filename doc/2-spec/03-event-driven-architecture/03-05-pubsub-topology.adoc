== Principles

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps the message bus small enough to understand and strict enough to trust?
****

Google Cloud Pub/Sub is the only bus.
Its topology follows five rules.

. *One command topic and one event topic per context that needs them.* Not one topic per message type: messages about the same order must stay in order, which only one topic can guarantee.
. *One subscription per consumer and per topic.* Each consumer reads at its own pace and fails on its own.
. *One dead-letter topic per subscription.* A message that cannot be handled is set aside where its consumer, and nobody else, can find it.
. *Everything is created by Terraform.* No topic or subscription is created by a service at startup, by hand or from a console.
. *Names carry the environment.* The three environments share one Google Cloud project, so every name starts with `test`, `integration` or `prod`.

.Settings of every topic
[%unbreakable, cols="4,4,8", options="header"]
|===
| Setting | Value | Reason
| Schema | The Protobuf schema of the topic, binary encoding | A message that does not match is refused at publication, before it can reach a consumer

The catalog, the cart and the notification services have no topic: they publish nothing (document 03-01).

== Subscriptions

.Strategy question
****
What strategy gives each consumer exactly the messages it handles, in order, with a way out for those it cannot handle?
****

.Subscriptions (the environment prefix is omitted)
[cols="5,3,3,5", options="header"]
|===
| Subscription | On topic | Consumer | Receives
| `inventory.commands.inventory-service` | `inventory.commands` | Inventory | Everything
| `inventory.events.order-service` | `inventory.events` | Order | Everything except {EVT-INV-007}
| `inventory.events.catalog-service` | `inventory.events` | Catalog | {EVT-INV-007} only
| `payment.commands.payment-service` | `payment.commands` | Payment | Everything
| `payment.events.order-service` | `payment.events` | Order | Everything
| `order.events.cart-service` | `order.events` | Cart | {EVT-ORD-001} only
| `order.events.notification-service` | `order.events` | Notification | Everything
|===

A subscription that does not receive everything has a *filter* on the attribute `ce-type`.
Filtering happens in Pub/Sub: the messages a consumer does not want are never delivered to it and cost it nothing.
A filter that excludes one type lets through any type added later.
That is expected: a consumer that receives a type it does not know acknowledges it and counts it (document 03-06).

.Settings of every subscription
[cols="4,4,8", options="header"]
|===
| Setting | Value | Reason
| Delivery | Pull, streaming | The consumer controls its own pace; nothing is exposed to receive pushes
| Message ordering | Enabled | Messages with the same ordering key are delivered one at a time, in order
| Acknowledgement deadline | 30 seconds, extended by the client while a message is being handled | Handlers are short; a message whose handler died is redelivered quickly
| Retry policy | Exponential backoff from 5 seconds to 5 minutes | A consumer in difficulty is not hammered
| Dead-letter topic | After 10 delivery attempts | A message that keeps failing stops blocking the messages behind it with the same key
| Message retention | 7 days | A consumer may be down for a weekend without losing anything
| Expiration | Never | A subscription must not disappear because its consumer was idle
| Exactly-once delivery | Disabled | Consumers are idempotent by design (document 03-06); the option would add latency without removing that need
|===

Ordering and dead-lettering work together in a way worth knowing: while a message is being retried, the messages behind it with the same key wait.
That is the desired behavior — an order's messages must not overtake each other — and it is why the number of attempts is bounded.

== Dead letters

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes sure a message that cannot be handled is noticed, kept and replayable?
****

Each subscription has its own dead-letter topic, named after it with the suffix `.dead-letter`, and that topic has one subscription used only for inspection and replay.

[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| What arrives there | A message that its consumer failed to handle 10 times. In a healthy system: nothing.
| Alert | Any message in a dead-letter subscription raises an alert within 5 minutes ({NFR-OBS-004}).
| Inspection | The message keeps its envelope: its type, its saga identifier and its trace context say which order it concerns and where it failed.
| Replay | Once the cause is fixed, the message is published again to its original topic by a documented procedure (document 06-07). Consumers being idempotent, replaying is safe at any time: beyond the retention of the inbox, a participant still answers from the status of its payment or reservation, and a request replayed after the instant it carries is not executed.
| Retention | 7 days. A dead letter not handled within a week is a process failure, and the alert is still firing.
|===

A dead letter on a saga's subscription usually means an order is stuck in a working state.
The review deadline covers it: the order goes under review and a person is already looking.

== Access rights

.Strategy question
****
What strategy ensures a service can publish and read only what the catalog of messages says it should?
****

Each service runs under its own Google identity, obtained through Workload Identity Federation: no key exists.
Rights are granted on individual topics and subscriptions, never on the project.

.Rights per service
[%unbreakable, cols="3,6,6", options="header"]
|===
| Service | May publish to | May read from
| Order | `inventory.commands`, `payment.commands`, `order.events` | `inventory.events.order-service`, `payment.events.order-service`
| Inventory | `inventory.events` | `inventory.commands.inventory-service`
| Payment | `payment.events` | `payment.commands.payment-service`
| Catalog | – | `inventory.events.catalog-service`
| Cart | – | `order.events.cart-service`
| Notification | – | `order.events.notification-service`
|===

Pub/Sub itself needs two rights to move dead letters: to publish to each dead-letter topic, and to acknowledge on the subscription the message came from.
They are granted to the Pub/Sub service agent by Terraform, with the rest.

== Creation and environments

.Strategy question
****
What strategy guarantees that the three environments have exactly the same topology?
****

The topology is declared once, as a Terraform module that takes the environment name as a parameter, and applied three times.
A topic, its schema, its subscriptions, their filters, their dead-letter topics and the rights are created together or not at all.

.What Terraform creates per environment
[%unbreakable, cols="6,3,6", options="header"]
|===
| Resource | Count | Note
| Schemas | 5 | One per topic, from the contract files
| Topics | 5 | With their schema
| Subscriptions | 7 | With filter, ordering, retry policy and dead-letter policy
| Dead-letter topics and their inspection subscriptions | 7 and 7 | –
| Access bindings | About 25 | Per topic and per subscription
|===

On a workstation and in component tests, the Pub/Sub emulator stands for the service.
It does not validate Protobuf schemas, so schema enforcement is verified against the real Pub/Sub of the test environment, by the integration tests of document 07-03.

At the volumes of this platform — a few messages per order — Pub/Sub costs almost nothing: the monthly free allowance is far above what a demonstration store publishes.
