== The questions to answer

.Strategy question
****
What strategy tells us what the platform is doing without opening a debugger in production?
****

Observability is judged by the questions it answers, not by the tools installed.

.Four questions
[cols="5,5,5", options="header"]
|===
| Question | Asked by | Answered with
| Is the store working for customers, right now? | The person on call | Service level objectives, measured at the entry point and on the order flow
| Where is this order, and what happened to it? | Support, an administrator, an engineer | The saga identifier, in logs and traces ({NFR-OBS-001})
| Why did this request fail? | An engineer | The trace of the request and the log lines that share its identifier
| What is about to break? | The person on call | Metrics of saturation and of backlog, with alerts ahead of the failure ({NFR-OBS-004})
|===

Services emit three signals — logs, metrics, traces — through one protocol, OpenTelemetry, and the managed services of Google Cloud store them ({ADR-PLT-028}).

plantuml::diagrams/telemetry-flow.puml[format=svg, align=center]

.Signals and their path
[%unbreakable, cols="2,6,6", options="header"]
|===
| Signal | Emitted as | Stored and read in
| Logs | One JSON object per line on standard output | Cloud Logging, collected by the cluster itself
| Metrics | An endpoint on the management port of each pod, in the Prometheus format | The managed Prometheus service, which scrapes it; Grafana for dashboards
| Traces | OTLP, from the OpenTelemetry support of Spring Boot {v-spring-boot}, to a collector in the cluster | Cloud Trace
|===

On a workstation one container, the Grafana OpenTelemetry stack {v-otel-lgtm}, receives the three signals: what an engineer sees locally has the same names and the same identifiers as in production.

== Identifiers that tie signals together

.Strategy question
****
What strategy lets one value pulled from a customer's e-mail lead to every log line, span and metric about their order?
****

.Identifiers
[cols="3,6,6", options="header"]
|===
| Identifier | Born | Carried by
| Trace identifier | At the entry point, for each request | The standard trace header on every HTTP call; the trace attribute of the envelope on every message (document 03-04). A consumer continues the trace of the message it handles.
| Saga identifier | With each saga, in the order service | The envelope of every command and answer of the saga; a field of every log line and an attribute of every span written while handling them (document 03-03)
| Order identifier and order number | With the order | Log fields and span attributes wherever an order is concerned. The number is what a customer quotes.
| Error code | Where the error is mapped | The error answer, the log line, a metric label (document 05-01)
| Version of the service | At build | A field of every log line and a label of every metric: an error rate can be tied to a release
|===

A message is published by the relay some time after the transaction that wrote it.
The trace context is saved with the message in the outbox and restored at publication, so that the trace of a request continues through the bus instead of stopping at the database.

The placement of an order is therefore one trace, from the checkout request through every message it causes.
A saga gets other traces when something else sets it in motion: a deadline that fires, a request of an administrator.
The saga identifier is what links them all; searching logs or traces on it gives the whole life of an order.

== Logs

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes a log something a machine can search and a person can trust?
****

.Fields of every log line
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Field | Content
| Time, severity | In UTC, to the millisecond
| Service, version, environment | Which code wrote the line, where
| Trace and span identifiers | Present on every line written while handling a request or a message
| Saga and order identifiers | Present when the work concerns an order
| Event | A stable name for what happened: `order.placed`, `saga.transition`, `outbox.parked`. What dashboards and log-based alerts match on, never the free text.
| Message | A sentence for a person
| Error code, exception | When there is one (document 05-01)
|===

.Rules
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| Format | Structured, always ({NFR-OBS-002}). No line is written as free text, including those of libraries.
| Levels | Information for what happened as expected, refusals included; warning for a failing dependency; error for a defect. A healthy system writes no error.
| A saga | Each transition writes one line: saga, order, state left, state entered, transition number, cause. The history of an order can be read from these lines alone.
| Never logged | A password, a token, a payment datum, the body of a request, an e-mail address or a postal address ({NFR-SEC-007}). The customer is named by identifier only.
| Masking | Fields marked as secret in a contract are masked by the logging configuration, by construction and not by the care of each author.
| Volume | Reads are not logged one by one in production: metrics and sampled traces cover them. Changes are.
| Retention | 30 days for operational logs. The audit of administration actions is kept one year, in its own log store ({NFR-SEC-006}); the business history — status changes, stock movements, price changes — lives in the databases.
|===

== Metrics

.Strategy question
****
What strategy chooses a few numbers that say whether things are well, instead of collecting everything?
****

.What every service exposes
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Family | Measures
| Requests served | Rate, errors and duration of each endpoint, by status ({NFR-OBS-003})
| Calls made | Rate, errors and duration of each outgoing call; state of each circuit breaker; refusals of each bulkhead
| Messages | Handled, refused, failed and redelivered, by type; time between publication and handling
| Outbox | Rows waiting, age of the oldest, rows parked; time between the saving of a message and its publication ({NFR-REL-005})
| Database | Connections in use and waiting; duration of statements
| Runtime | Memory, garbage collection, threads, restarts
|===

.What the business exposes
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Family | Measures
| Orders | Placed; settled by outcome and by reason; time from placement to outcome ({NFR-PERF-003})
| Sagas | In progress by state; suspended by remembered step; settled by a late answer; events recorded with no effect; compensations sent and completed; defensive transitions fired (document 03-03)
| Checkout | Accepted; refused by error code
| Payments | By outcome: captured, refused, unknown, canceled, refunded; refunds failed
| Stock | Reservations held, rejected, expired; delay between a stock change and the catalog ({NFR-PERF-005})
| E-mails | Sent, retried, not sent
|===

.Rules
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| Names | One naming scheme for all services, set by the shared starter. A metric means the same thing everywhere.
| Labels | From small, closed sets only: service, endpoint, status, outcome, reason, error code. Never an order, a customer or a SKU: such values belong to logs and traces.
| Durations | Histograms, with boundaries placed at the objectives, so that "under 300 ms" is read exactly.
| Bus | The age of the oldest message of each subscription and the content of each dead-letter subscription come from Pub/Sub's own metrics.
|===

== Traces

.Strategy question
****
What strategy shows where the time of a request went, across services and across the bus?
****

.Rules
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| Spans | One per request served, per outgoing call, per database transaction, per message published and per message handled. Created by the framework's instrumentation, not by hand.
| Attributes | The saga and order identifiers, the transition of a saga, the error code. Never personal data.
| Across the bus | The context travels in the envelope; the handling of a message is a child of its publication.
| Sampling | Every trace that places, cancels or decides an order is kept, with everything it causes: these are few and they are the ones that matter. Reads are sampled at 10 % in production. Everything is kept in the test and integration environments. The decision is taken once, where a trace starts — the web gateway for a request, the task for a deadline — and every service that continues the trace follows it.
| Errors | A sampled-out request that fails is still found: its log lines carry the trace identifier, and the error answer gives it to the person.
| Retention | 30 days.
|===

== Service level objectives

.Strategy question
****
What strategy replaces "is everything green?" by a number the business agreed to?
****

An objective states, for a kind of request, what share must be good over a month.
What is left is the _error budget_: the room for failures and for risk.

.Objectives
[cols="4,6,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| Function | A request is good when | Objective | Requirement
| Catalog, product and cart reads | It is answered without a server error | 99.5 % | {NFR-AVAIL-001}
| Order history and order detail | It is answered in less than 300 ms | 95 % | {NFR-PERF-001}
| Publication of a saved message | It is published in less than 5 s | 99 % | {NFR-REL-005}
| Same | It is answered in less than 300 ms; 200 ms for the cart | 95 % | {NFR-PERF-001} {NFR-PERF-004}
| Checkout | A valid checkout is accepted | 99.0 % | {NFR-AVAIL-005}
| Checkout | It is acknowledged in less than 500 ms | 95 % | {NFR-PERF-002}
| Order outcome | The order is settled in less than 5 s; 15 s | 95 %; 99 % | {NFR-PERF-003}
| Displayed availability | A stock change reaches the catalog in less than 5 s | 95 % | {NFR-PERF-005}
|===

Availability is measured where a customer would see the failure: on the request metrics of the load balancer, which still count when no pod of ours answers, and by a probe that calls the storefront and a catalog read from outside every minute.
Durations of the order flow come from the saga metrics.
A refusal of the business is a good answer: a checkout refused because a price changed did not fail.

.Using the error budget
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Situation | Action
| The budget burns fast: over the last hour, and still over the last 5 minutes, at a rate that would spend it in two days | The person on call is paged
| The budget burns slowly: over the last 6 hours, and still over the last 30 minutes, at a rate that would spend it before the end of the month | A ticket is opened for the next working day
| The budget of a month is spent | Deliveries of features to the function concerned stop until it is restored; reliability work comes first
|===

== Alerts

.Strategy question
****
What strategy wakes someone only when a person must act, and tells them what to do?
****

An alert is raised on what customers suffer or on what will make them suffer soon, never on a cause that may be harmless.
Each alert names its runbook (document 06-07).
An alert that asks for no action is deleted.

.Alerts
[cols="5,5,2,5", options="header"]
|===
| Alert | Condition | Urgency | Protects
| Error budget burning fast | Any objective above | Page | Customers, now
| Error budget burning slowly | Any objective above | Ticket | The month
| Orders under review | Any order under review for more than 30 minutes; or more than 10 at once | Page | {NFR-REL-003}
| An order is put under review | Each occurrence | Ticket | {NFR-OBS-004}
| Dead letter | Any message in a dead-letter subscription | Page | An order stuck, a consumer broken
| Outbox late | Oldest unpublished row older than 60 s; or any parked row | Page | {NFR-REL-005}
| Defensive transition | A capture on a rejected order, or a refund on a confirmed one | Page | Money held or returned wrongly
| Payment provider unavailable | Circuit open for more than 5 minutes | Page | Order placement
| Refund failed | Each occurrence | Ticket | A customer waiting for money
| E-mail not sent | Each occurrence after the last attempt | Ticket | {FR-NOT-003}
| Subscription backlog | Oldest unacknowledged message older than 2 minutes | Ticket | A consumer slow or down
| Database connections | Above 80 % of the budget for 10 minutes | Ticket | The next outage
| Pod restarting | A pod restarted more than 3 times in 10 minutes | Ticket | A defect or a wrong limit
| Unexpected errors | Each occurrence of the generic code | Ticket; page if the rate is sustained | A defect shipped
| Invalid requests | An abnormal rate on one endpoint | Ticket | A defect of the storefront, or an attack
| Probe failing | Two consecutive failures of the outside probe | Page | The store unreachable while our own metrics see nothing
| Access refused | A burst of refused sign-ins or of forbidden requests | Ticket | An attack in progress (document 05-04)
|===

Every alert is evaluated often enough to fire within 5 minutes of its condition ({NFR-OBS-004}), and is tested in the test environment by provoking that condition.
Spending alerts are in document 06-06.

== Dashboards

.Strategy question
****
What strategy gives each reader one screen that answers their question?
****

.Dashboards
[%unbreakable, cols="4,5,6", options="header"]
|===
| Dashboard | For | Shows
| Objectives | The person on call, the product owner | Each objective, its budget left, its burn rate
| Order flow | Support, the product owner, engineers | Orders placed and settled by outcome and reason; time to outcome; sagas by state; orders under review
| One per service | Its engineers | Requests, calls made, messages, outbox, database, runtime; the version deployed
| Bus | The person on call | Backlog and age per subscription; dead letters
| Platform | The platform engineers | Pods, restarts, scaling, capacity (document 06-03)
|===

Dashboards and alert rules are files of the repository, reviewed and deployed like code.
A dashboard edited by hand in production is overwritten at the next deployment.

== Health and diagnosis

.Strategy question
****
What strategy lets the platform and an engineer ask a service how it is, without exposing that to the world?
****

[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| Management port | Health, metrics and build information are served on a port of their own, which the web gateway does not route and only the cluster reaches.
| Health | Three answers, used by the platform as document 05-02 specifies: started, ready, alive.
| Information | The version, the commit and the build time of what is running.
| In development only | A local console shows beans, mappings and configuration of a running service. It is never part of an image that is deployed.
| No shell in production | Diagnosis relies on signals. Nobody connects to a pod to look.
|===
