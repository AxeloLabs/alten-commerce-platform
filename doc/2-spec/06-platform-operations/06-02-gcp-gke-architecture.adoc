== The project at a glance

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps the cloud side of the platform small enough to draw on one page?
****

Everything runs in one Google Cloud project and one region ({ADR-PLT-026}).
The platform uses managed services wherever one exists, so that the only thing the team operates is its own software.

.Resources of the project
[cols="4,4,8", options="header"]
|===
| Resource | Service | Role
| Cluster | GKE Autopilot | Runs the web gateway, the six services and the identity provider, in three namespaces
| Entry point | Global external Application Load Balancer, declared through the Gateway API | One public address; TLS; routing by host name ({ADR-PLT-019})
| Firewall at the edge | Cloud Armor | Rules against web attacks; rate limits (document 05-04)
| Certificates | Certificate Manager | Issued and renewed automatically for the host names of the three environments
| Names | Cloud DNS | The public zone of the store
| Databases | Cloud SQL for PostgreSQL {v-postgresql} | One instance, highly available, private address ({ADR-PLT-012})
| Messages | Pub/Sub | Topics, subscriptions, schemas (document 03-05)
| Documents | Firestore | Only where a service is configured for it: the contract tests of the catalog and the cart ({ADR-PLT-013})
| Secrets | Secret Manager | Document 05-04
| Images | Artifact Registry | One repository, in a location that spans several regions; images signed and attested by the pipeline
| Admission of images | Binary Authorization, with a key in the key management service | Only attested images run (document 06-05)
| Outgoing traffic | Cloud NAT | One known address for calls to real providers. Nothing uses it while both providers are simulated inside the platform.
| Observability | Cloud Logging, Cloud Trace, Managed Service for Prometheus | Document 05-03
| Outside probe, alert channels | Cloud Monitoring | The probe and the notifications of document 05-03
| Access to internal screens | Identity-Aware Proxy | Grafana and the administration console of the identity provider, for named people only
|===

.Fixed choices
[%unbreakable, cols="4,4,8", options="header"]
|===
| Choice | Value | Reason
| Region | `europe-west1` | Three zones, every service above available, a low price tier. A configuration value: any region with three zones suits.
| Zones | Three | The cluster and the database spread over them ({NFR-AVAIL-002})
| Creation | Terraform only | Nothing is created by hand or by a service (document 06-04)
|===

== The network and its addresses

.Strategy question
****
What strategy gives every component an address that says where it is and who may reach it?
****

One private network holds everything.
No node and no pod has a public address; two public addresses exist in all, one to come in and one to go out.

.Network of the platform: ranges and addresses
plantuml::diagrams/network.puml[format=svg, align=center, pdfwidth=78%]

.Address plan
[cols="4,3,8", options="header"]
|===
| Range | Addresses | Use
| `10.10.0.0/24` | 256 | Nodes of the cluster: the primary range of the subnet
| `10.20.0.0/17` | 32,768 | Pods, of the three namespaces together: a secondary range of the subnet
| `10.30.0.0/22` | 1,024 | Kubernetes services: a secondary range of the subnet
| `10.40.0.0/24` | 256 | Managed services reached privately: the database instance
| `10.50.0.0/16` to `10.90.0.0/16` | – | Reserved for the company layout of document 06-01: one block per future environment or network, so that ranges never overlap
| `199.36.153.8/30` | 4 | Google APIs through private access. Fixed by Google.
| `35.191.0.0/16`, `130.211.0.0/22` | – | The load balancer and its health checks, as seen by the pods. Fixed by Google.
|===

.Addresses that matter
[%unbreakable, cols="4,4,7", options="header"]
|===
| Address | Kind | What it is
| `203.0.113.20` | Public, reserved, global | The store: the host names of the three environments resolve to it
| `203.0.113.10` | Public, reserved, regional | The address our calls to providers come from: what a provider would put on its allow list
| `10.40.0.3` | Private | The database instance
| `10.30.0.10` | Private | The name service of the cluster
| `169.254.20.10` | Local to each node | The name service as a pod sees it: a cache on its node, in front of the one above
| `169.254.169.254` | Local to each node | The metadata server, on ports 80 and 8080: where a pod obtains the credential of its identity
| A pod | Private, from the pod range | Changes at every restart. Nothing refers to a pod by address: services are reached by name.
|===

The two public addresses above are examples from the range reserved for documentation; the real ones are reserved by Terraform and published in its outputs.
Private ranges are final.

.Names
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Name | Resolves to
| For each environment, the host name of the store and that of its identity provider | The public address. The load balancer routes on the host name: the store to the web gateway of that environment, the other to the sign-in pages of its identity provider and to nothing else of it (document 05-04).
| `<service>.<namespace>.svc.cluster.local` | The service of that name in that environment: how the web gateway reaches a service, and a service another. A name never crosses a namespace.
| The Google APIs | The private-access range: calls to Pub/Sub, the secret manager and telemetry never leave Google's network
|===

In the proof of concept no domain name is bought.
The host names come from a free public service that resolves a name containing an address to that address, and the certificate is requested for those names.
Should the provider refuse a certificate for such names, the proof of concept runs with a certificate signed by ourselves, and the browser warns its visitors: this is to be tried at the first deployment.

.Why pods of three environments share one range
****
Namespaces are not networks: the pods of `test` and `prod` take their addresses from the same range.
What separates them is the network policy of each namespace, which denies everything by default and then allows named neighbors of the same namespace (document 05-04).
A range per environment belongs to the company layout, where each environment has its own cluster.
****

== The three paths of traffic

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes it obvious which traffic may come in, which may go out, and which never leaves?
****

plantuml::diagrams/request-path.puml[format=svg, align=center]

.Paths
[cols="3,8,6", options="header"]
|===
| Path | Route | Controls
| Incoming | Browser, Google's front end at the public address, then straight to a pod of the web gateway: the load balancer knows the pods themselves, not the nodes | TLS ends at the front end; Cloud Armor filters; only the web gateway and the sign-in pages of the identity provider are behind the load balancer
| Private | A pod to the database on its private address; a pod to the Google APIs through private access | No public address involved. Each call is authenticated by the identity of the workload.
| Outgoing | A pod to a provider, through Cloud NAT | Outgoing only: nothing can open a connection inwards through it. Network policies allow it for the payment and notification services only.
|===

.Firewall rules of the network
[%unbreakable, cols="5,10", options="header"]
|===
| Rule | Content
| Default | Incoming traffic is denied.
| From the load balancer | Allowed from Google's two ranges to the pods of the web gateway and of the identity provider, on their serving ports. Created with the Gateway.
| Between pods | Decided by network policies, per namespace.
| Administration access to nodes | None. Nodes are managed by Autopilot; nobody connects to them.
|===

== The cluster

.Strategy question
****
What strategy gives a small team a production-grade cluster without a platform team to run it?
****

GKE Autopilot is imposed by the brief.
Google manages the nodes, their upgrades and their scaling; the team declares workloads and pays for what they request.

.Settings of the cluster
[cols="4,4,8", options="header"]
|===
| Setting | Value | Reason
| Mode | Autopilot | Imposed. No node pool to size or patch.
| Location | Regional, three zones | The control plane and the workloads survive the loss of a zone
| Version | Release channel {v-gke-channel}, Kubernetes {v-kubernetes} at the time of writing | Upgrades are automatic, within a maintenance window outside business hours
| Nodes | Private | No node has a public address
| Control plane | Reached by the pipeline and by operators through its authenticated endpoint | No administration from an arbitrary address
| Network policies | Enforced | The separation of namespaces depends on it
| Identity of workloads | Workload Identity Federation for GKE | No key files (document 05-04)
| Gateway API | Enabled, with the class of the global external load balancer | The entry point is declared like any other Kubernetes object
| Secret Manager integration | Enabled | Secrets mounted as files
| Managed Prometheus | Enabled | Metrics collected without anything to run
| Admission of images | Binary Authorization in blocking mode: only images that the pipeline attested | Document 06-05
|===

.What Autopilot imposes, and what it changes for us
[cols="5,10", options="header"]
|===
| Constraint | Consequence
| Every container states the processor and memory it requests | Requests are what is billed and what is guaranteed. They are sized in document 06-03 and watched in document 06-06.
| No privileged container, no access to the node | Tools that inject faults or inspect the kernel cannot be installed (document 05-02). Our own workloads need neither.
| Nodes appear when pods need them | The first pod of an environment scaled to nothing starts in tens of seconds, not instantly. Acceptable for `test` and `integration`; production never scales to nothing.
| Pods can be moved for maintenance | Every service tolerates the loss of a replica: two replicas, a disruption budget, a clean shutdown (document 05-02).
|===

.Namespaces
[%unbreakable, cols="3,12", options="header"]
|===
| Namespace | Content
| `test`, `integration`, `prod` | The web gateway, the six services and the identity provider of that environment; its network policies, quotas and service accounts
| `edge` | The Gateway: the one entry point, with a route per environment
| `observability` | The collector of traces; Grafana, reached through the identity-aware proxy by named people. No Grafana in the proof of concept.
|===

== Managed services

.Strategy question
****
What strategy sets up each managed service so that it is private, recoverable and no larger than needed?
****

.Cloud SQL
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Choice
| Engine | PostgreSQL {v-postgresql}
| Availability | A primary and a standby in two zones, with automatic failover (document 05-02)
| Address | Private only, in the range reserved for managed services
| Access | Through the Cloud SQL connector, with the Google identity of each service; a database account per service and per environment, with a limit on its connections (document 04-04)
| Databases | One per service and per environment, plus those of the three identity providers and of the three web gateways
| Backups | Daily, and recovery to a point in time over the last 7 days ({NFR-AVAIL-004}). Stored in a location that spans several regions, so that they survive the loss of the region (document 06-07)
| Size | Full profile: two processors, eight gigabytes of memory, 500 connections accepted, storage that grows by itself; reviewed after the load tests. Proof of concept: a small shared instance without standby, 60 connections (documents 06-03 and 06-06).
| Maintenance | A window outside business hours
|===

.Other services
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Service | Setup
| Pub/Sub | Per environment: 5 topics with their schema, 7 subscriptions, 7 dead-letter topics with their inspection subscriptions (document 03-05). Storage in the region.
| Secret Manager | One secret per need and per environment; versions; access per identity
| Artifact Registry | One repository, in a location that spans several regions, so that images survive the loss of the region; tags cannot be moved once pushed. Images are scanned by the pipeline, not by the registry (document 06-05).
| Certificate Manager | One certificate for the six host names, validated through the DNS zone
| Cloud Armor | One policy on the entry point: managed rules, rate limits, a stricter limit on sign-in and checkout. Not in the proof of concept.
| Cloud NAT | One reserved address, for the payment and notification services only, the day they call real providers. In this release both providers are simulated inside the platform, in every environment: nothing calls out, and the proof of concept does not create the address.
| Firestore | One database in native mode, used by the contract tests of `test`; none in production as long as PostgreSQL is the store
|===

== Rights

.Strategy question
****
What strategy grants each identity exactly what it uses, and nothing broader?
****

.Rights of a service identity
[cols="4,6,5", options="header"]
|===
| Need | Right | Granted on
| Publish its messages | Pub/Sub publisher | Each topic it publishes to
| Read its messages | Pub/Sub subscriber | Each of its subscriptions
| Connect to the database | Cloud SQL client and instance user | The project, restricted by a condition to the instance; the database account then limits it to its own database
| Read its secrets | Secret accessor | Each of its secrets
| Send traces and metrics | Trace agent, metric writer | The project
|===

Three rights exist only at the level of the project: connecting to the database instance, sending traces, sending metrics. None of them gives access to any data.
Logs need no right: they are written to standard output and collected by the cluster.

.Other identities
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Identity | Rights
| The build pipeline | Push images to the registry; create attestations with the key of the attestor. Authenticated from GitHub without a stored key (document 06-05).
| Deployment, one per environment | Change the workloads of its namespace. Issued only to the job of the pipeline bound to that environment.
| Migrations, one per environment | Change the schema of the databases of its environment. Used by the migration jobs of the charts.
| Operations jobs, one per environment | What the jobs of document 06-07 need, and no more: publish to the topics and read the dead-letter subscriptions of its environment, move a subscription in time, restart or scale a workload of its namespace, call the administration interface of the identity provider
| Trace collector, Grafana | Write traces; read metrics
| Terraform | Create and change the resources of this document, from the pipeline only
| Engineers | Read logs, metrics and traces of every environment; nothing else in production
| Emergency access | Granted for a limited time, on request, and recorded
|===

== How the layout grows

.Strategy question
****
What strategy names today the limits of this layout and the step that removes each one?
****

[cols="5,5,5", options="header"]
|===
| Limit of this release | Step that removes it | When it becomes worth it
| Production shares a database instance with tests | A second instance for `test` and `integration` | Before real customers, or the first time a load test disturbs production
| One cluster for three environments | A cluster per environment | Before real payments
| One region | A second region for production, the database replicated, the entry point already global | When an outage of a region costs more than a second region
| Traffic between pods is not encrypted | Transparent encryption between nodes, or a service mesh | When a rule or an audit requires it
| Encryption keys are managed by Google | Keys managed by us | When a rule or an audit requires it
| A pod can reach the Google APIs of any project | A service perimeter around the project | Before real customer data
| Engineers reach the control plane from anywhere, authenticated | Access from a private network only | With the company layout
|===

Each step is a change of the infrastructure code and a migration, not a redesign: names, identities and the per-environment module already allow it (document 06-01).
