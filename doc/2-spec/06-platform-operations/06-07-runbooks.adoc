== How an incident is handled

.Strategy question
****
What strategy lets someone woken at night do the right thing without having designed the system?
****

Every alert of document 05-03 names a runbook of this document.
Three runbooks have no alert of their own and start from the judgment of a person: R16, R18 and R19.
A runbook does not explain the architecture; it says what to look at and what to do, in order, and when to stop and call someone.

.The same six steps, whatever the incident
[cols="1,3,11", options="header"]
|===
| # | Step | Meaning
| 1 | Acknowledge | Say that you have it, so that nobody else starts in parallel.
| 2 | Measure the harm | Two dashboards: objectives, and order flow. Are customers affected, how many, since when?
| 3 | Stop the harm | Before understanding. If a release went out in the last hour, return to the previous one first (document 06-05).
| 4 | Say it | One message where the team looks: what is seen, what is being done, when the next news comes.
| 5 | Repair | Follow the runbook. Change nothing by hand in production: every action below is a button of the administration, a job of the pipeline, or a command reserved to the owner.
| 6 | Close | Check the signal that fired is back to normal; write down what happened while it is fresh.
|===

.Urgency
[%unbreakable, cols="3,6,6", options="header"]
|===
| Level | Means | Expected
| Page | Customers are harmed now, or money or data is at risk | Someone acts within minutes, at any hour
| Ticket | Something will become a problem, or one customer is affected | Handled the next working day
|===

== Operations jobs

.Strategy question
****
What strategy repairs production without ever typing a command against it?
****

Document 06-06 forbids changing production by hand.
Repairs that need more than a button of the administration are therefore _operations jobs_: small programs kept in the repository and tested like the rest.
Each is started as a manual run of the pipeline, bound to the environment it targets — hence, for production, to an approval — and executed in the cluster under the operations identity of that environment, which can do what the jobs need and nothing else (document 06-02).
Each run is recorded: who asked, with what parameters, what it did.

.Operations jobs
[cols="4,7,4", options="header"]
|===
| Job | Does | Used by
| Wake or sleep an environment | Sets the replicas of every workload of `test` or `integration` to their normal value, or to nothing | Pipelines; the evening schedule; level 1 of the budget procedure
| Replay dead letters | Publishes the messages of one dead-letter subscription again to their original topic, with their envelope, then acknowledges them | R02
| Release a parked message | Clears the parked mark of one outbox row, after its cause is fixed; or discards it, with a reason | R04
| Send an e-mail again | Sends once more an e-mail recorded as not sent | R07
| Replay a consumer from a point in time | Moves one subscription back to an instant, so that a repaired consumer handles again what it mishandled | R02, after a defect that acknowledged messages wrongly
| Restart a workload | Replaces the pods of one workload, one at a time | R11, R18
| Quarantine a workload | Scales one workload to nothing, and back | R19
| End sessions | Ends the sessions of named accounts at the identity provider and requires a new password at their next sign-in | R17
| Create an administrator | Creates an account with the role `ADMIN` at the identity provider | First setup
| Reconcile after a restore | Lists the payments and reservations whose order is unknown to the order service or is in an earlier state, and the reverse | R14
| Close and reopen the store | Closes the entry, waits until no order is in progress, scales production to nothing; and the reverse | Level 2 of the budget procedure
| Restore drill | Clones the database instance at a past instant, checks the clone, deletes it | Routine procedures
| Load synthetic data | Fills an empty `test` or `integration` | After a rebuild
|===

Every job relies on what the design already guarantees: consumers are idempotent, so replaying a message that was handled does nothing (document 03-06).

== Runbooks for the order flow

.Strategy question
****
What strategy brings every order to an outcome when the system could not get there alone?
****

[cols="1,4,6,7", options="header"]
|===
| # | Alert | Look at | Do
| R01 | Orders under review, many or old
| The order-flow dashboard: under review by remembered step. Which service was silent? Is it back?
| If a service is down, restore it first: orders whose answer was missing settle by themselves when it returns (document 02-03). For those that remain, an administrator decides order by order in the administration: retry, confirm or reject. Never change an order in the database.

| R02 | A message in a dead-letter subscription
| The message: its type, its saga identifier, its trace. The logs of the consumer for that trace: why did it fail ten times?
| Fix the cause: usually a defect, so a release. Then run the job that replays the dead letters of that subscription. The order concerned is under review meanwhile, by its deadline: after the replay, check that it settled (R01).

| R03 | Outbox late
| The dashboard of the service: is the relay running? Is the database answering? Is Pub/Sub accepting messages?
| A relay stopped with its pod restarts with it. If Pub/Sub refuses everything, there is nothing to do but wait: messages are saved and will leave in order. Tell the team: every order is delayed.

| R04 | A parked message in an outbox
| The reason recorded on the row: schema, size, right to publish.
| A parked row blocks the later messages of the same order for the same topic, on purpose. Fix the cause — a schema and a release that disagree, a missing right — then run the job that releases the row. Discarding it is a decision of the product owner, since a message of an order is lost.

| R05 | A capture on a rejected order, or a refund on a confirmed order
| The order, its saga, its payment. This should never happen: a defect exists.
| For a capture on a rejected order, the refund was asked automatically: check that it arrived. If it failed, the owner refunds at the payment provider and the fact is recorded on the order. For a refund on a confirmed order, the product owner decides with the customer: cancel the order, or take the payment again. In both cases open a defect at the highest priority.

| R06 | A refund failed
| The order, under review; the reason given by the payment provider.
| Retry in the administration when the provider works again. If it keeps failing, contact the provider with the payment reference. Tell the customer what is happening: they are waiting for money.

| R07 | An e-mail not sent
| The reason; the state of the e-mail provider.
| When the provider works again, run the job that sends the e-mail again. The order itself is unaffected.
|===

== Runbooks for services and dependencies

.Strategy question
****
What strategy restores service fast when the cause is not yet known?
****

[cols="1,4,6,7", options="header"]
|===
| # | Alert | Look at | Do
| R08 | Error budget burning
| Which objective, since when. Was there a release? Is a dependency failing: database, payment provider, identity provider?
| A release in the last hour: return to the previous one, then investigate. A dependency: go to its runbook. Load: check that replicas were added; if they are at their maximum, the entry point is limiting requests as designed, and the team decides whether to raise a maximum (document 06-03).

| R09 | Probe failing: the store cannot be reached from outside
| The load balancer: are pods of the web gateway healthy? The certificate, the name of the store, the firewall: is it blocking everyone?
| No healthy pod: R11. A certificate or a name: they are managed and renew themselves; a failure there is a change of ours, to revert. A firewall rule blocking legitimate traffic: revert the change that introduced it.

| R10 | Payment provider unavailable
| The circuit breaker of the payment service; the provider's own status page.
| Nothing to repair on our side. Orders placed meanwhile are not charged; they are rejected when the provider returns, and shown under review if that takes more than a few minutes. Tell the team and, if it lasts, the customers. Afterwards: R01 for what remains.

| R11 | A pod restarting, or a rollout that does not finish
| The events of the pod and its last log lines: a setting or a secret missing, memory exceeded, a migration failed, an image refused?
| A rollout that fails stops by itself and the previous version keeps serving: let the pipeline return to the previous release. Memory exceeded on a version that was fine: a leak or a load never seen; raise the request by a reviewed change, not by hand.

| R12 | A subscription backlog
| The consumer: are its pods ready? Is it failing on one message, or slow on all?
| One message failing: it will become a dead letter (R02). Slow on all: look at what it calls — database, provider — and go to that runbook.

| R13 | Database connections high
| Which service holds them; slow statements.
| A defect that keeps connections, or a maximum of replicas raised without revisiting the budget of document 06-03. Return to the previous release, or lower the maximum, by a reviewed change.

| R14 | The database failed over, or must be restored
| The instance: is the standby serving? Is data damaged?
| A failover needs nothing: services resume within a minute. Damaged data — a faulty release wrote wrong rows in one database — is repaired without touching the others. The instance is cloned as it was just before the damage, into a temporary instance that only the procedure can reach. The damaged database alone is copied from the clone into the live instance, during a short stop of its service. The clone is deleted. At most 5 minutes of that service's data are lost ({NFR-AVAIL-004}). The service is then behind its neighbors: it publishes again what its outbox held, the others answer from their status (document 03-06), and the reconciliation job lists what an administrator must still settle. The owner starts the procedure.

| R15 | The identity provider is down
| Its pods; its database.
| Restore it like any workload (R11). Meanwhile nobody can sign in and, within five minutes, signed-in users can only browse; sessions resume when it returns (document 05-04).

| R16 | The region is lost
| The provider's status. Nothing of ours answers.
| Wait if the outage is announced as short. Otherwise rebuild in another region: apply the infrastructure code with the other region, restore the database from its backups, deploy the last release, point the name of the store to the new address. Backups, images and the states of the infrastructure are kept in locations that span regions for this purpose. What was written since the last backup — up to a day — is lost, and messages in flight with it: the 5 minutes of {NFR-AVAIL-004} cover the loss of the instance, not of the region. Hours, not minutes: an accepted risk (document 05-02).
|===

== Runbooks for security and money

.Strategy question
****
What strategy limits the damage of an intrusion or of an overspend in the first quarter of an hour?
****

[cols="1,4,6,7", options="header"]
|===
| # | Alert | Look at | Do
| R17 | A burst of refused sign-ins or of forbidden requests
| Where from; which accounts; which addresses.
| Tighten the rate limit, or block the source, at the firewall of the entry point, by a reviewed change applied at once. If accounts were entered: end their sessions at the identity provider and force a new password.

| R18 | A secret was exposed
| Which secret, where, since when.
| Treat it as stolen, even if the exposure was removed. Add a new version, restart the workloads that use it, disable the old version (document 05-04). Then look at the audit log for its use during the exposure.

| R19 | A workload behaves as if taken over
| What it called, what it published, since when.
| Scale it to nothing: its work waits in its subscriptions. Keep its logs. Rotate what it could read. Rebuild it from a clean image; review what it wrote to its database and to its topics before letting consumers continue.

| R20 | A budget alert
| The breakdown of spending by environment and by service; what changed since yesterday.
| Proof of concept: destroy it now, without waiting for its deadline. Full profile: follow the four levels of document 06-06; levels 2 to 4 are for the owner. Find the cause before waking anything again.

| R21 | Unexpected errors, or an abnormal rate of invalid requests
| The traces of the errors: which release, which endpoint, which input.
| An unexpected error is a defect: open it with its trace, and return to the previous release if customers are affected. Invalid requests coming from our own storefront are a defect of the storefront; coming from elsewhere, go to R17.

| R22 | Drift: the nightly plan is not empty, or the inventory lists a resource the code did not create
| What differs; who did it, in the audit log.
| If the change was right, make it in the code. In every case let the pipeline bring reality back to the code. A resource nobody declared is removed once its author is found.
|===

.Commands reserved to the owner, for levels 2 to 4
[source,shell]
----
# Proof of concept — destroy everything now, without waiting for the deadline.
#   Run the pipeline job "destroy the proof of concept".
#   Lost: the data of the demonstration. Kept: the repository, the project, the bucket of the states, the budget.
#   If the pipeline itself cannot run, the last resort removes the project and everything in it:
gcloud projects delete <project>
#   Lost: everything, the states and the budget included; the project can be restored for 30 days.

# Level 2 — close the store.
#   Run the operations job "close the store" on prod, with the owner's approval:
#   it closes the entry, waits until no order is in progress, then scales production to nothing.
#   Lost: nothing if the store reopens within 7 days. Beyond, the bus drops the messages still waiting.
#   Kept: data and configuration. Untouched: the database, the two other environments.

# Level 3 — stop the database instance.
#   Lost: nothing. Kept: all data and backups. Billed: storage only. Every environment stops working.
gcloud sql instances patch <instance> --activation-policy=NEVER
#   To restart:
gcloud sql instances patch <instance> --activation-policy=ALWAYS

# Level 4 — detach billing. Last resort.
#   Everything stops at once. Resources may be deleted by the provider if billing stays off.
#   Untouched: the repository, from which the platform can be rebuilt; backups only as long as the project lives.
gcloud billing projects unlink <project>
----

== Routine procedures

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps rare procedures from being improvised the day they are needed?
****

[cols="4,4,8", options="header"]
|===
| Procedure | When | How
| Wake and sleep an environment | Every evening, and around test runs | The operations job. Production only by the owner.
| Rotate a secret | On a schedule, and after any suspicion | New version, progressive restart, old version disabled
| Restore drill | At a fixed rhythm, in the full profile | The operations job clones the instance at a past instant into a temporary instance that only the job can reach — it holds production data and stays under the rules of production — checks that each database opens and that its counts match, times the whole, deletes the clone, keeps the report
| Failover drill | Rarely, in an announced window outside business hours | There is one instance: the drill fails over production too, for about a minute, and that is what it proves. Never automatic, never nightly. Impossible in the proof of concept, which has no standby.
| Rebuild an environment | After a change of the infrastructure modules | The job _rebuild integration_ of document 06-04, after an approval; then load synthetic data
| Destroy the proof of concept | At its deadline, automatically; earlier at the owner's request | The job of document 06-04; then read the list of what remains in the project
| Review of costs | Weekly | Spending by environment and by service against the estimate (document 06-06)
| Review of exceptions | Monthly | Vulnerabilities accepted with an expiry date (document 06-05); emergency accesses granted
| Upgrade of the cluster | Automatic | Read the notes of the new version when it reaches `test`; nothing else, unless an alert fires
|===

== First setup of a project

.Strategy question
****
What strategy turns an empty cloud account into a working platform, with the few manual steps in the right hands?
****

Done once, by the owner of the project (document 06-04).

[cols="1,7,7", options="header"]
|===
| # | Step | Why by the owner
| 1 | Create the project and link it to a billing account | Spending authority
| 2 | Apply the `owner` root of the infrastructure code: the budget, its thresholds and who is alerted | The first guardrail exists before anything can spend, and only the owner has rights on the billing account
| 3 | Create the bucket of the infrastructure states, with versions | The tool needs it before it can create anything
| 4 | Establish the trust between the repository and the project, and the identity of the pipeline with its rights | A pipeline cannot grant itself rights
| 5 | Full profile only: register the domain name of the store, and delegate its zone | A purchase
| 6 | Run the pipeline: the four infrastructure roots, then a first release | From here on, everything is automatic
| 7 | Give the secrets that come from outside their first value. None in this release: both providers are simulated | Only the owner would hold them
| 8 | Create the first administrator account, with the operations job | The role `ADMIN` is never obtained by a screen of the store
|===

Steps 1 to 5 can be done from the command line; the exact commands are kept with the infrastructure code, next to the module they prepare, and are run by a person, not by an agent.
In the proof of concept, step 6 is the run in which the owner gives the deadline of destruction (document 06-06).

== After an incident

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes each incident the last of its kind?
****

Every page, and every incident a customer noticed, is followed by a short written review within a week.
It looks for causes in the system, never for a culprit.

.Content of a review
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Part | Content
| What happened | The timeline, from the first signal to the return to normal, with the identifiers of traces and sagas
| The harm | Who was affected, for how long, what it cost of the error budget
| Why | The causes, down to why the system allowed them
| What worked, what did not | Did the alert fire in time? Did the runbook help? What had to be improvised?
| What changes | Actions with an owner and a date: a test, an alert, a guardrail, a line of a runbook
|===

A runbook that had to be improvised is corrected the same week: this document is expected to change after every incident.
