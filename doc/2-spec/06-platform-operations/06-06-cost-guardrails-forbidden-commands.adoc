== Two kinds of accident

.Strategy question
****
What strategy protects a cloud project from the two accidents that cannot be undone: a bill and a deletion?
****

A cloud platform can hurt its owner in two ways that code review does not catch: by spending without limit, and by destroying something that cannot be rebuilt.
Both are prevented the same way, in three layers.

.Three layers of guardrail
[%unbreakable, cols="3,6,6", options="header"]
|===
| Layer | For the bill | For destruction
| Prevent | Ceilings on what can be requested; environments that sleep; nothing sized "just in case" | People and agents lack the right; the resource itself refuses deletion
| Detect | A budget with alerts on what is spent and on what is forecast | A nightly check that nothing was changed by hand (document 06-04); the audit log
| Correct | A written procedure that stops spending, level by level | Backups, recovery to a point in time, infrastructure rebuilt from code (document 05-02)
|===

Two facts shape this document.
*A budget does not stop anything*: it sends alerts, and services keep running and keep being billed.
And *the platform is first deployed as a proof of concept*, for two days, under a budget of 10 EUR ({ADR-PLT-030}): its first guardrail is not an alert but a deadline, after which everything is destroyed.

== What the platform costs

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes the bill predictable, to the hour, before the first resource is created?
****

Nothing is paid for writing or planning infrastructure.
A resource costs from the second it is created to the second it is destroyed; the bill is a rate per hour multiplied by a duration.
The rate depends on the profile that is deployed ({ADR-PLT-030}).

.Unit prices used below
[cols="5,4,6", options="header"]
|===
| Item | Public price | Note
| Pods on Autopilot | 0.0445 USD per processor and per hour; 0.0049 USD per gibibyte of memory and per hour | Billed by the second, on what pods request
| Cluster | 0.10 USD per hour | Covered by a monthly credit of 74.40 USD, given once per billing account: free as long as this is its only cluster
| Database, small shared instance | 0.035 USD per hour | The instance of the proof of concept. Not covered by the provider's availability commitment.
| Database, dedicated instance | From 0.0413 USD per processor and 0.007 USD per gigabyte, per hour; doubled with a standby | The instance of the full profile
| Entry point | 0.025 USD per hour for the first five rules; 0.008 USD per gibibyte processed | –
| Messages, secrets, images, logs, traces, names | Inside the free allowances at our volume | –
| Outgoing address, managed firewall | Not checked | Absent from the proof of concept; small lines of the full profile
|===

Prices are those published by the provider on 2026-11-19, for a reference region; they vary a little by region and change over time.
The price of the dedicated database comes from an older page of the provider and is the least certain line.
Every figure below is a computation from this table, not a quotation: it is to be redone with the provider's calculator before the first deployment.

== The budget and its alerts

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes sure the owner learns of an overspend from an e-mail, not from an invoice?
****

.The budget ({NFR-COST-001})
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| Scope | The project, all services included
| Amount | 10 EUR per month: the decision of the owner on 2026-11-19. A parameter of the infrastructure code.
| Alerts on what is spent | At 50 %, 90 % and 100 % of the amount
| Alert on the forecast | When the spending forecast for the month exceeds the amount, whatever was spent so far
| Who is told | The owner, by e-mail, at every threshold
| Unusual spending | The provider's detection of anomalies is on
|===

A budget alert is a late signal: the provider computes spending with a delay of several hours, sometimes a day.
Over a deployment of two days it can arrive after the money is spent.
In the proof of concept it is therefore the second net; the first is the deadline of the next section.

The budget lives in a root of the infrastructure code of its own, `owner`, applied by the owner and not by the pipeline: it concerns the billing account, whose rights are not delegated (document 06-04).

== Destruction on a deadline

.Strategy question
****
What strategy guarantees that a proof of concept stops costing money even if everyone forgets it?
****

.The deadline ({ADR-PLT-030})
[cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| Given when | The owner starts the deployment of the proof of concept and gives, with it, the instant after which nothing must remain: 48 hours later by default. No deployment starts without a deadline.
| Kept where | With the state of the infrastructure, readable by the pipeline
| Checked by | A scheduled run of the pipeline, every half hour. The scheduler of the hosting platform can be late by some minutes: the deadline is honored within the hour.
| At the deadline | The pipeline destroys every resource of the project that the infrastructure code created: workloads, cluster, database and its data, topics, entry point, images. It then lists what is left in the project and sends the list to the owner.
| Before the deadline | The owner can run the same destruction at any time, or move the deadline, by starting the job with a new instant
| What survives | The repository, from which everything can be created again by one run; the project itself, the bucket of the states and the budget, none of which is billed
| What is lost | The data of the demonstration. This is intended.
|===

== Limits that prevent

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes a runaway bill impossible rather than merely noticed?
****

[cols="4,6,5", options="header"]
|===
| Limit | Value | Stops
| Maximum replicas per service | Those of document 06-03: one in the proof of concept | Scaling without end under an attack or a defect
| Quota of each namespace | A little above the sum of its maxima | A wrong chart asking for a hundred replicas
| Quota of the project in the region | Lowered to what the profile can use | Resources created outside the namespaces, by mistake or by an intruder
| Sleeping environments, in the full profile | `test` and `integration` are scaled to nothing every evening ({NFR-COST-002}) | Paying nights and week-ends for nothing
| Database | A fixed size; storage that grows by itself, up to a ceiling | A table that fills the disk
| Logs and traces | Retention of 30 days; reads sampled; no debug level in any environment | A volume that grows with traffic
| Images | Only the last releases are kept | Storage that only grows
| Requests from outside, in the full profile | Rate limits at the entry point (document 05-04) | An attacker paying with our money
| Kinds of resource | No accelerator, no large machine class, no resource outside the region | The expensive mistakes
|===

The proof of concept has no rate limit at its entry: the managed firewall is one of the things it does without.
Its exposure is bounded differently: one replica per service that cannot scale, quotas, and two days of existence.

== When the budget is reached

.Strategy question
****
What strategy stops the spending at the right level: enough to stop the bleeding, no more?
****

In the proof of concept the answer is one action: the owner runs the destruction without waiting for the deadline.
The levels below are for the full profile, where there is something to keep.

plantuml::diagrams/kill-switch.puml[format=svg, align=center, pdfwidth=85%]

.Four levels, in the full profile
[cols="1,5,4,5", options="header"]
|===
| # | Action | Who | Effect
| 1 | Scale `test` and `integration` to nothing, and close the spending latch | Automatic at 90 %; anyone on the team before | Nothing is lost. Tests wait.
| 2 | Close the store: close the entry, let orders in progress finish, then scale production to nothing | The owner | Data and configuration are intact. Messages wait 7 days at most: reopened later than that, pending cancellations and refunds would be lost.
| 3 | Stop the database instance | The owner | Nothing can start; only its storage is billed; it restarts in minutes with its data
| 4 | Detach billing from the project | The owner, as a last resort | Everything stops at once. Resources may be deleted by the provider if billing stays off.
|===

.The spending latch
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| What it is | A marker, kept with the guardrails of the project, that says "do not wake anything"
| Who closes it | A small function subscribed to the messages of the budget: at 90 % it closes the latch and scales `test` and `integration` to nothing. It has the right to do that and nothing else.
| Who obeys it | Every pipeline reads the latch before waking an environment, and stops with a clear message if it is closed. A merge during an overspend builds and tests, and deploys nothing.
| Who opens it | The owner, after finding the cause
|===

Levels 2 and 3 are procedures of the runbook (document 06-07).
Level 4 is not automated, on purpose: the provider documents a way to trigger it from a budget alert, and it would let a miscalculated forecast destroy a store that has customers.

== Commands nobody runs

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps a tired person, or an eager agent, from typing the one command that cannot be taken back?
****

The commands below are forbidden to engineers and to AI agents alike.
Forbidding is not enough: for each family the table says what makes the command fail even if someone tries.

.Destroying
[cols="6,5,5", options="header"]
|===
| Never | Why | What stops it
| Deleting the project, or detaching its billing | Ends everything | Only the owner has the right
| Deleting the database instance, a database, a backup | The data of the store | Deletion protection; no right for engineers
| Deleting the cluster or a namespace | Every workload of one or three environments | Deletion protection; the pipeline alone has rights on the cluster
| Deleting a topic or a subscription, or moving a subscription back or forward in time outside the runbook | Messages lost or replayed blindly | Protected in the infrastructure code; no right for engineers
| Deleting a secret or destroying its versions | A service that can no longer start | No right for engineers
| Deleting images that a release refers to | A rollback becomes impossible | The retention rule keeps them; no right for engineers
| `terraform destroy`, outside the two destruction jobs of document 06-04; removing or editing the state by hand | The description and the reality part ways | Engineers cannot write the state; the two jobs are reviewed code, and the one that reaches production data exists in the proof of concept only
| Dropping or emptying a table, deleting rows without a condition, in production | Data | Nobody has a session on the production database; the service accounts cannot change the schema
|===

.Opening
[cols="6,5,5", options="header"]
|===
| Never | Why | What stops it
| Creating a key file for a service identity | A credential that lives for ever and can be copied | Identities are federated; the creation of keys is disabled where the project allows it
| Granting a broad role — owner, editor — to anyone or anything | Least privilege ends there | The roles that matter are managed exclusively by the infrastructure code: a grant made by hand is removed by the next run, and a nightly inventory lists every resource the code did not create (document 06-04)
| Giving the database a public address, or opening a firewall rule to every address | The data on the internet | The security scan of the infrastructure code refuses it
| Making a bucket, a topic or an image public | The same | The same
| Disabling the admission policy of images, or a network policy | The door is open to the next mistake | Both are in code; the nightly check sees it
|===

.Bypassing
[cols="6,5,5", options="header"]
|===
| Never | Why | What stops it
| Changing a workload by hand in `integration` or `prod`: applying, editing, scaling, deleting, installing or rolling back a chart | Nobody reviewed it, nobody can reproduce it | Engineers can read the cluster, not write to it
| Opening a shell in a pod of production | Diagnosis relies on signals (document 05-03); a shell sees secrets and personal data | Not granted
| Applying infrastructure from a workstation, or to a part only | It skips the review and the order of environments | Engineers have no right on resources or on the state. One exception, written down: the `owner` root, applied by the owner
| Forcing a push to the main branch, rewriting its history, skipping the checks of a commit | The main branch is what gets deployed | Branch protection
| Putting a secret in a file, a variable of the pipeline, a commit message, a ticket | It will be found | The secrets scan; a leaked secret is rotated (document 05-04)
| Running a script downloaded from the internet in the pipeline or with production rights | Someone else's code with our identity | Steps of the pipeline are pinned and few (document 06-05)
| Copying production data to another environment or to a workstation | Personal data leaves its place | No right to read production data
|===

.The commands themselves, by tool
[cols="2,13", options="header"]
|===
| Tool | Forbidden
| `gcloud` | `gcloud projects delete` · `gcloud billing projects unlink` · `gcloud sql instances delete` · `gcloud sql databases delete` · `gcloud sql backups delete` · `gcloud container clusters delete` · `gcloud pubsub topics delete` · `gcloud pubsub subscriptions delete` · `gcloud pubsub subscriptions seek` · `gcloud secrets delete` · `gcloud secrets versions destroy` · `gcloud iam service-accounts keys create` · `gcloud projects add-iam-policy-binding` · `gcloud artifacts docker images delete` · `gcloud services disable` · `gcloud storage rm` on the bucket of the states
| `kubectl` | `kubectl delete namespace` everywhere. In `integration` and `prod`: `kubectl apply`, `edit`, `patch`, `scale`, `delete`, `rollout undo`, `exec`, `cp`, `port-forward`. Everywhere: `kubectl create secret`
| `helm` | `helm install` · `helm upgrade` · `helm rollback` · `helm uninstall`
| `terraform` | `terraform destroy` outside the destruction jobs · `terraform apply` outside the pipeline, except the `owner` root by the owner · `terraform apply -target` · `terraform state rm`, `mv`, `push` · `terraform force-unlock` · `terraform import` outside a reviewed change
| `git` | `git push --force` to the main branch · `git commit --no-verify` · any rewriting of the history of the main branch
| SQL, in production | `DROP` · `TRUNCATE` · `DELETE` or `UPDATE` without a condition · any change of schema outside a migration
|===

The runbook of document 06-07 uses some of these — stopping the database instance, moving a subscription in time, restarting or scaling a workload — inside written procedures: as commands of the owner, or as operations jobs run by the pipeline.
Outside those procedures they are forbidden.

.What is always allowed
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Action | For
| Listing and describing resources; reading logs, metrics and traces | Everyone on the team, in every environment
| Computing a plan of the infrastructure | The pipeline, and engineers with read-only rights
| Rendering a chart; running the whole platform on a workstation | Everyone
| Everything, in `test` | Engineers, for an investigation: `test` is rebuilt from the repository
|===

For AI agents the same lists are enforced a second time, by the rules of the harness that decide which commands an agent may run without asking, with asking, or never (document 08-02).
