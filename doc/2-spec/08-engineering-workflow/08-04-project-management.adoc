== How the project is steered

.Strategy question
****
What strategy tells, at any moment, what is done, what comes next and what is at risk, without anyone maintaining a second plan by hand?
****

The specification is the plan.
It already says what to build, in which order (document 08-03), and which tests prove each part (document 07-02).
Project management adds only what a specification cannot hold: the state of the work, today.

.Four principles
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Principle | Meaning
| One plan | The backlog is generated from the specification. Nobody types a story or a task that the specification does not contain; to change the plan, one changes the specification.
| Done is measured | An item is done when the pipeline says so: its tests pass. Nobody declares a percentage.
| One place | Backlog, code, review and delivery are in the same tool, linked by the identifiers of this specification
| Small steps, few at a time | An increment is a pull request. Finishing one comes before starting another.
|===

.What is kept where
[%unbreakable, cols="4,5,6", options="header"]
|===
| Thing | Place | Answers
| What to build, and why | The specification, in the repository | What is the plan?
| Units of work and their state | GitHub Issues ({ADR-PLT-032}) | What is in progress, what is blocked?
| A change | A pull request | What exactly was done, and who reviewed it?
| The view | One GitHub Project | Where are we in the milestone?
| The truth about "done" | The pipeline (document 06-05) | Does it work?
|===

The brief suggested Jira.
The repository, the reviews and the pipeline are on GitHub, and agents drive it from the command line: a second product would add an account, a connector and a place where the state can differ.
Should an employer impose Jira, the mapping below applies unchanged; only the tool differs.

== The backlog

.Strategy question
****
What strategy keeps the backlog and the specification from telling two different stories?
****

plantuml::diagrams/backlog-model.puml[format=svg, align=center, pdfwidth=52%]

.From the specification to the backlog
[cols="3,5,7", options="header"]
|===
| Item of the specification | Entry of the backlog | Closed when
| Epic | An issue, parent of its stories | All its stories are closed
| User story | An issue, sub-issue of its epic. It lists the use cases that realize it and the increments that build them. | The acceptance scenarios of its use cases pass in the pipeline; for a story on a critical path, its end-to-end test passes on a deployed environment
| Creation prompt | An issue: the unit of work. Its body is the filled prompt of document 08-03. | Its pull request is merged
| – | A defect: the identifier of what is violated, and the test that shows it | The pull request that makes the test pass is merged
| – | A gap of the specification, reported by an agent or a person | The specification is corrected, or the product owner accepts it as it is
| – | A risk of the register below | The risk is gone, or accepted
|===

.How an entry is written
[%unbreakable, cols="3,12", options="header"]
|===
| Field | Rule
| Title | The identifier, then the title of the specification: `CRE-ORD-002 — Place-order saga`. The identifier is what links everything; it is never left out.
| Kind | A label: `epic`, `story`, `increment`, `defect`, `spec-gap`, `risk`
| Context | A label from the context codes of this specification: `ctx:ord`, `ctx:cat`…
| Priority | For stories, the priority of the specification: `must`, `should`, `could`
| Critical path | A label, for stories and defects that touch one. They are handled first.
| Milestone | The milestone of document 08-03
|===

.Generated, not typed
[%unbreakable, cols="3,12", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| Source | The registry of identifiers that the build of this specification produces, the same that feeds document 00-03: each epic, story and creation prompt with its title and its links
| Tool | A script of the repository, run by a person, that creates or updates the issues through the command line of GitHub. It finds an existing entry by the identifier in its title, so that running it twice changes nothing.
| Direction | From the specification to the backlog, never back. A title or a link is corrected in the specification, and the script is run again.
| What the script never touches | The state of an entry, its comments, its assignee: those belong to the people who work
| An identifier that disappears | Its entry is closed as not planned, with a reference to the change of the specification
|===

.The two commands the script is made of
[source,shell]
----
gh issue create --title "CRE-ORD-002 — Place-order saga" \
  --label "increment,ctx:ord" --milestone "M1 — Services" \
  --body-file build/prompts/CRE-ORD-002.md

gh project item-add <project-number> --owner <owner> --url <issue-url>
----

Creating about a hundred issues in a repository is visible to everyone who watches it.
The script is therefore run on purpose, by the owner of the repository, once the specification is approved.

== The board

.Strategy question
****
What strategy shows the state of the work at a glance, to someone who was not there yesterday?
****

One project holds every entry.

.States
[%unbreakable, cols="3,8,4", options="header"]
|===
| State | Meaning | Who moves it
| Backlog | Exists, not yet startable | The script
| Ready | Meets the definition of ready below | The developer
| In progress | A branch exists | The developer
| In review | A pull request is open | The developer, or an automation of the project
| Blocked | Waits for a decision or for a correction of the specification. The reason is written on the entry. | The developer
| Done | Merged; for a story, proven by the pipeline | An automation of the project, when the entry is closed
|===

.Views
[%unbreakable, cols="3,12", options="header"]
|===
| View | Shows
| Current milestone | A board of the increments of the milestone, by state. The daily view.
| By service | A table grouped by context: the parallel tracks of document 08-03
| Critical paths | The stories and defects that carry the label: what must never wait
| Stories | The stories by epic, with what remains to build for each
| Roadmap | The milestones in time, once the pace is known
|===

.Limits on work in progress
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Limit | Reason
| One increment in progress per track | An agent works on one increment at a time on one branch
| At most three increments in review | Review is the scarce resource: a person reads every change. When three wait, nothing new starts.
| A blocked entry is looked at the same day | A gap of the specification stops a track until it is settled
|===

== Milestones and scope

.Strategy question
****
What strategy turns forty-three increments into a few moments where something can be shown and decided?
****

.Milestones
[cols="2,6,7", options="header"]
|===
| Milestone | Can be shown | Proves
| M0 — Foundations | A pull request going through every gate; the local platform starting | The conventions hold before any business code depends on them
| M1 — Services | Each service through its interface, on a workstation | Every business rule, in unit and component tests; the acceptance scenarios in memory
| M2 — Saga | The nineteen saga scenarios passing, failures provoked | {CP-ORD-001} to {CP-ORD-004} below the browser: the heart of the brief
| M3 — Storefront | The purchase in a browser, on the local platform | The six critical paths end to end; {CP-CAT-001} and {CP-IAM-001}
| M4 — Delivery | The platform on GKE, deployed by the pipeline | The proof of concept; the cost guardrails
|===

A milestone is reached when the release auditor of document 08-03 finds nothing missing and the product owner accepts it.

.If time is short: what to cut, in this order
[cols="1,6,8", options="header"]
|===
| # | Cut | Price
| 1 | The document and file stores: {CRE-CAT-003}, {CRE-CART-003} | The brief's requirement of three interchangeable stores is specified and not demonstrated
| 2 | The longer load runs and the dashboards of {CRE-PLT-016} and {CRE-PLT-017} | Performance is specified, measured by one short run only
| 3 | Cancellation and fulfillment: {CRE-ORD-003}, and their parts of {CRE-ORD-006}, {CRE-ORD-009} and {CRE-ORD-010} | Two validated journeys are absent; no critical path is affected
| 4 | The administration screens, replaced by calls to the interfaces during the demonstration | The administrator's journeys are shown without a screen
|===

What is never cut: the foundations, the saga and its scenarios, the security of the edge, the cost guardrails.
They are what the brief is about.

.The first deployment
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Rule
| When | After M3, for the demonstration. Before that, everything runs on a workstation and in the pipeline, at no cloud cost.
| Rehearsal | One deployment of a few hours, some days earlier, to meet the surprises of a first time — a certificate, a quota, a right. At the hourly cost of document 06-06, three hours cost about half a dollar.
| Procedure | The first-deployment steps of the runbook (document 06-07): the budget first, by the owner
| End | The scheduled destruction, at the deadline given when the environment is created ({ADR-PLT-030})
|===

== Ready and done

.Strategy question
****
What strategy prevents starting what cannot be finished, and calling finished what is not?
****

.Definitions
[cols="3,6,6", options="header"]
|===
| For | Ready when | Done when
| An increment | Its row exists in the catalog of document 08-03; the documents it reads are approved; the increments before it are merged; no gap of the specification is open on its subject | Its pull request is merged: every gate passes; the tests it names exist under their identifiers; each finding of an expert is fixed or dismissed with a reason; the report of the author is read, and each gap it mentions has an entry
| A story | Its use cases and acceptance scenarios are approved | The acceptance scenarios of its use cases pass in the pipeline; on a critical path, its end-to-end test passes on a deployed environment
| A defect | It names the identifier that is violated and how to see it | A test that failed because of it passes, and stays in the suite
| A milestone | – | The release auditor reports nothing missing; the product owner accepts it
|===

"It works on my workstation" is not a state of the board.

== Working agreements

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes every change traceable to the reason it was made?
****

[cols="3,12", options="header"]
|===
| Subject | Agreement
| Branch | One per increment, named after it: `cre-ord-002-place-order-saga`. Short-lived.
| Commit | Small and frequent, on the branch. The message starts with the identifier of the increment or of the defect.
| Pull request | One per increment. Its description follows a template: the identifier; what was built; each test identifier and its state; the assumptions and the gaps of the specification met; the findings of the experts and what was done about each.
| Review | By a person, always (document 08-02). In a team, not the person who launched the agent.
| Merge | Only with every gate passing. The pull request closes its entry of the backlog by naming it.
| A gap of the specification | Never settled in the code. An entry is opened, the track waits or takes the most conservative reading, and the specification is corrected by its own pull request.
| A decision | A choice between alternatives that was not in the specification becomes a decision record (document 04-07), in the same pull request as the specification change
| Versions | A dependency is upgraded by an increment of its own, never in passing
|===

== Roles

.Strategy question
****
What strategy makes clear who decides what, when one person and several agents hold every role?
****

.Who decides
[cols="4,7,4", options="header"]
|===
| Decision | Decided by | Proposed by
| Scope, priorities, accepting a milestone | The product owner | Anyone
| Spending: creating the environment, its deadline, the budget | The owner of the cloud account | {role-cloud}
| Architecture: a new decision record | {role-arch} {role-sol} | Anyone, an agent included
| Merging a change | The reviewer | The author
| Dismissing a finding of an expert | The reviewer, with a written reason | –
| Deploying to production | The approver of the environment (document 06-05) | The pipeline
| Changing a gate, a threshold, the harness | The owners named on those files | Anyone
|===

An agent proposes, builds and reviews.
It decides nothing in this table and is accountable for nothing: accountability stays with the person who launched it (document 08-02).

In the proof of concept one person holds every role of the role tags of this specification.
The tags still matter: they say which hat is worn when a decision is taken, and which checklist applies.

== Risks

.Strategy question
****
What strategy deals with what can go wrong before it does?
****

.Register, at the start of the construction
[cols="5,4,6,2", options="header"]
|===
| Risk | First sign | Response | Owner
| The mutation tool does not run on JUnit {v-junit} | The gate fails to start in {CRE-PLT-001} | Tried in the very first increment. If it cannot run: the gate is suspended, written down as such, and the test reviewer of document 08-03 carries the question until it can. | {role-qa}
| The bus emulator behaves unlike the real bus | A scenario passes in containers and fails in `test` | The behaviors that differ — schemas, order, dead letters — are tested on the real bus ({TI-PLT-004}) | {role-swe}
| More code is written than one person can read | Increments wait in review | Increments sized for one sitting; at most three in review; experts read first | {role-arch}
| The specification has gaps found during construction | Entries labeled `spec-gap` accumulate | Agents must report, never fill silently; the specification is corrected first; a track may wait | {role-sol}
| The demonstration date comes before M4 | The pace measured at M0 and M1 | The ordered cuts above; never the foundations | Product owner
| The environment is left running | A budget alert | Destruction on a deadline, checked every half hour; alerts at half, nine tenths and all of the budget (document 06-06) | {role-cloud}
| The certificate or the host name of the proof of concept is refused | The first deployment | The rehearsal; a certificate signed by ourselves as a fallback (document 06-02) | {role-net}
| A new minor release of Spring Boot during the project | Its announcement | Versions are pinned; the upgrade is an increment of its own, after the demonstration | {role-arch}
| One developer | Unavailability | Everything is in the repository: specification, prompts, backlog. Another person, or another agent, can take the next increment. | Product owner
| An agent does something it should not | A refused command in the telemetry; a surprising change | The harness of document 08-02: the agent holds nothing with which to harm the cloud | {role-sec}
|===

The register is reviewed at each milestone: risks that passed are closed, and what the milestone revealed is added.

== Reporting

.Strategy question
****
What strategy reports progress with numbers that nobody had to estimate?
****

.At each milestone
[%unbreakable, cols="4,11", options="header"]
|===
| Number | Source
| Increments merged, of 43 | The board
| Stories done, of 39, and of those on critical paths | The pipeline: acceptance scenarios passing
| Identifiers with a passing test, by type | The traceability matrix of document 00-03, joined with the test reports
| Findings of experts: fixed, dismissed, open | The pull requests
| Gaps of the specification: found, corrected | The backlog
| Delivery measures | Document 06-05
| Cost so far, once an environment exists | The billing export (document 06-06)
|===

.Forecasting
****
No duration is estimated in this specification: nobody has yet built a platform with this factory.
The pace is measured during M0 — increments merged per day of work — and the forecast is the number of increments left divided by that pace.
It is stated as a range, and corrected at each milestone.
****
